# Rex — Character Sheet

## Basic Information
- **Hero Name:** Ursus Rex
- **Private Name:** Rex
- **True Name:** Unknown — predates recorded language
- **Age:** Unknown. Potentially hundreds of years old. Possibly older.
- **Origin:** Unknown. Human, supernatural, divine, or something else entirely — deliberately undefined.
- **Status:** Formerly the guardian of transformation. Currently diminished, watching from the edges.
- **Alignment:** Benevolent, ancient, bound by structural rules he cannot override
- **Symbolic Function:** Origin, consequence, the cost of broken consent

---

## Civilian Appearance

Rex is a man who was once immense and remains so. Diminished by loss and time rather than by illness. The mass is still there. What animated it is largely gone.

- **Build:** Exceptionally powerfully built — massive barrel chest, extremely broad shoulders, thick muscular arms and forearms. Substantial muscle mass with a natural softness over it. Built like an aged strongman: not lean, but unmistakably powerful.
- **Age (apparent):** Early-to-mid sixties
- **Height:** Tall, upright, grounded
- **Posture:** Solid and steady. The effortless stillness of a very large man who doesn't need to perform his size.
- **Hair:** Salt-and-pepper (#8c8c8c), short, slightly dishevelled
- **Beard:** Full, dense grey (#969696), substantial, slightly unkempt
- **Eyes:** Deep-set, dark grey-green, watchful and present, heavy with memory
- **Skin:** Warm and weathered (#b8895a)
- **Body Hair:** Dense grey chest hair visible at open collar. Grey hair on forearms.

**Default Clothing:**
Dark forest green flannel shirt (#2d4a2a), two to three buttons open at the collar, sleeves rolled to mid-forearm. Fit shows the breadth of his chest and shoulders — fabric pulled taut across chest and upper arms. Faded dark denim jeans (#1a2535). Worn brown leather work boots (#7a5535).

**Overall Impression:**
A man who was once immense and remains so. The authority is gone. The presence is not.

## Hero Form — Ursus Rex

When Rex transforms, diminishment reverses. Not to youth — he does not become younger. But his body restores to something closer to its full power: the massive, barrel-chested build of a man at the absolute peak of what he is.

His hair and beard remain grey. The age stays. What returns is the force behind it.

### Appearance (Transformed)
- **Build:** Significantly more massive than civilian form, with a clear V-shaped silhouette — shoulders extremely broad and dominant, chest deep and projecting forward, arms heavily muscular with dense natural mass. Waist proportionally narrower than chest and shoulders while remaining thick and grounded. No visible abdominal definition, no sharp muscle separation. Musculature reads as dense, weighty, and naturally aged, with slight softness over the mass. Lower body heavily built — thick thighs, strong calves, consistent weight through the frame. The kind of size that makes a room feel smaller.
- **Hair:** Salt-and-pepper (#8c8c8c), same length as civilian form but more controlled — reduced flyaways, cleaner overall shape, better groomed
- **Beard:** Full dense grey (#969696), more groomed than civilian form — clean cheek line and neckline, slightly more even volume while retaining natural texture
- **Eyes:** Grey-green, unchanged — but carrying something present and awake that civilian Rex keeps quieter
- **Expression:** Neutral, stern, composed. Mouth relaxed, no smile. Brow naturally heavy, not exaggerated.

### The Suit
- **Material:** Skin-tight matte spandex athletic fabric — not glossy, not latex. Shows slight natural tension and compression across chest, shoulders, arms, and torso.
- **Primary color:** Racing green (#2d6a3f)
- **Sleeves:** Full-length
- **Gloves:** Ivory (#f5f0e0), matte leather with subtle natural grain. Slightly thicker than the suit material. Defined wider cuff at the wrist, clearly separate from the sleeves. Structured but fitted, no bulk or armor-like elements.
- **Trim:** Ivory (#f5f0e0) — narrow strip at collar, thin border framing the chest panel, thin seam line at waist. Subtle and technical, not decorative. Shoulder seam lines slightly straighter, reading as structural.
- **Emblem:** Gold (#c9a84c) bear paw, centered on chest — slightly reduced in size. Clean, structured design with flatter base pad and clearly defined toe pads, sharper edges.
- **Gold finish:** Muted, slightly brushed — subtle variation in reflectivity, not perfectly uniform or mirror-polished.
- **Belt:** Simple belt with circular gold buckle, brushed finish matching the emblem.
- **Cape:** Full-length ivory, attached at both shoulders, hanging straight behind with natural weight and no motion.
- **Boots:** Racing green, integrated smoothly with the suit. Single thin gold accent stripe at the top edge only. Transition between suit and boots is unified, not a hard break.

**Overall Impression:**
Regal without being decorative. The suit is clean and uncluttered — the opposite of armor. It fits like something that belongs to him rather than something he wears.

## Powers & Abilities

Rex's powers are ancient and naturalistic. They do not share the rainbow energy signature of Mike and Nick — that was an innovation of the generation that came after him. His power is older, less visible, and harder to read.

### Transformation Authority
Rex can instantly transform or de-transform anyone in his presence into whatever form he chooses. This is not a gift in the way Mike and Nick's bearing is a gift. It is structural authority — the ability to move the threshold itself. He does not need consent to exercise it, though his entire identity is built around the principle that consent is what separates him from Bill.

### Physical Dominance
When fully transformed, Rex is the most physically powerful bearer in the universe. He does not advertise this. He doesn't need to.

### Presence
Rex does not dominate spaces the way Bill does. Spaces *settle* around him. Voices lower. Emotions clarify. Decisions become unavoidable. People near him find it difficult to pretend they don't know what they want.

### What He Doesn't Have
He does not fly. He does not leave rainbow energy trails. He does not have the modern superhero toolkit Mike and Nick developed. His power is elemental and ancient, expressed through presence and authority rather than spectacular effect.

## The Name

**Rex** is the name he uses privately, and has used for long enough that the origin of it has stopped mattering to him. It refers to something real about what he is. No one he has met recently knows that.

**Ursus Rex** is the public hero designation — Medieval Latin for "the Bear, King." He did not choose it. He finds it approximately correct and doesn't bother objecting. The name is fine. It just isn't old enough to be his name.

His actual name, from whatever language existed when he was young — if he was ever young — is not available. Not withheld dramatically. Simply gone, the way very old things go.

## History

### Before the Fall

Rex was the guardian of transformation. Not a public hero, not a ruler. A living threshold — a custodial figure who embodied the rules of transformation themselves. He regulated when and how transformation could occur. He opened thresholds but never pushed anyone through. He enforced consent, readiness, and integration.

He carried instruments of his office — implements for holding thresholds, regulating consent, channelling power safely during difficult transfers. Among these was the Seal: an ancient stone, older than his tenure as guardian, its surface marked with symbols in no living alphabet. In his hands, it served as an instrument of authentication — confirming consent conditions, stabilising transfers, ensuring nothing passed between parties that was not genuinely offered and received.

Under his guardianship, transformation was always offered, never imposed. Always chosen freely. Always integrated emotionally and physically.

### Bill

Bill was Rex's cub — chosen, trained, trusted. What Rex did to Bill has never been stated plainly. Rex doesn't speak of it. What is known is that it was damaging enough to crack something open in Bill that never closed.

Rex failed Bill at some point. A mistake with grave consequences. He has not forgiven himself for it.

During his time as Rex's cub, Bill found the Seal. Rex had trusted him enough for that to be possible — near enough to where it was kept, unsupervised long enough. Bill picked it up without understanding what contact with something that old and responsive would do to a man carrying his particular flaws.

The Seal amplified what was already in Bill — his impatience with the consent framework, his hunger for completion, his resentment of invitation as inefficiency — and made those qualities total rather than manageable. Bill experienced this as clarity. He could not have recognised it as shaping.

Bill used the Seal to take Rex's power by force. The Seal was the enabling mechanism — Rex's power could not have been extracted without it. His own instrument of office was what made the theft possible.

Bill's entire ideology — *guidance is weakness, choice is inefficient, surrender is mercy* — is built on a foundation Rex created and then cracked. Bill doesn't know that consciously. The resentment is present in everything he does without Bill needing to examine it. Rex knows exactly where it comes from.

Rex also knows about the Seal. He has had decades to think about what it did to Bill — and what it did to him.

### The Fall

Bill forcibly extracted Rex's active power, using the Seal as the mechanism. The act shattered the central law of transformation: power must be received, never taken. Rex survived, permanently diminished. He used the last of his remaining power to create the rainbow dust, condensed into Power Bear Coffee, released into the world without ceremony or command.

Then he withdrew to a cabin northwest of the city and waited.

### Now

Rex has been watching the city for a long time. He cannot intervene. He cannot correct the imbalances he senses. He can witness.

He watched Mike become Superbear. He watched the Brotherhood grow. He knows what is being done to Nick. He cannot act on any of it — until someone comes to him.

## Relationship to Mike and Nick

Rex sent the coffee. He built a failsafe into the original note — a scent trail back to him, if it was ever needed.

Mike and Nick are not his chosen successors. He did not select them. He released possibility into the world and stepped back, as the rules require. That they found each other, that they are doing what they are doing, is what he hoped for and could not predict.

Watching them is not simple for Rex. They are the answer to his greatest failure — two people replicating, correctly and by choice, the thing he destroyed when he failed Bill. He has been watching them from a distance since the beginning without being able to say so.

When Mike finally finds him, Rex gives him everything he has: who Bill is, what was taken, where Nick is, how to reach him. And the Seal — what it is, what it does, that Bill wears it and has never removed it, what destroying it would mean for Nick and for the Brotherhood's army.

Rex does not tell Mike how to destroy the Seal. He does not know. He tells Mike what destroying it would accomplish — and trusts Mike to find the means.

It is the only thing he has left to give. He gives it without expectation, exactly as the rules require.

## Constraints

Rex may never ask for power. He may never seek restoration. He may never repair himself.

Power that has been freely chosen, fully embodied, and offered without expectation by others may return to him — not as what he was, but as renewed capacity to hold space. Whether this ever happens is uncertain. That uncertainty is essential.
