# Nick — Character Sheet

## Basic Information
- **Name:** Nick
- **Age:** Early 30s
- **Sexuality:** Gay
- **Location:** Small apartment in a large city
- **Life Status:** Financially stable, emotionally stagnant

---

## Appearance (Before Transformation)
Nick is of **average height** with a **slim, slightly scrawny build**. He has learned, consciously or not, to take up as little space as possible.

- **Racial / Ethnic Background:** White North-American, Scandinavian descent
- **Skin Tone:** Fair / light
- **Posture:** Slightly hunched, closed-off
- **Hair:** Blonde, average thickness, perpetually messy and unstyled
- **Facial Structure:** Soft face with a gentle, non-imposing jawline
- **Nose:** Straight and unremarkable
- **Lips:** Average, often pressed together in uncertainty
- **Eyes & Expression:** Tired eyes; avoids eye contact, which makes him seem shy and nervous
- **Facial Hair:** Clean-shaven, though he sometimes wishes he could grow a beard
- **Body Hair:** Very little; smooth chest and arms — something he quietly wishes were different
- **Clothing:**
  - Plain basics (t-shirts, jeans)
  - Baggy clothes that don’t fit quite right
  - Wears the same hoodie most days, almost like armor

**Overall Impression:**
Nick is deliberately forgettable. He blends into crowds by instinct, appearing smaller and more hesitant than he really is.

---

## Personality
- Awkward and insecure
- Quietly empathetic
- Morally sensitive
- Conflict-avoidant
- Thoughtful, introspective

Nick cares deeply, even when it costs him comfort. He often feels like the odd one out for *still* caring.

---

## Work & Routine
- **Job:** Office worker reviewing insurance claims
- **Original Motivation:** Wanted to help people
- **Reality:** Spends his days finding technical reasons to deny aid

Nick hates his job, not because it’s boring, but because it goes against his values. His coworkers seem unfazed by the ethical implications, which only deepens his sense of isolation.

This job represents everything he feels trapped by:
- Stability without meaning
- Safety without fulfillment
- Doing what was expected, not what was right

---

## Core Wound
Nick always did what was expected of him.

He followed the prescribed path:
- Went to college
- Got a degree
- Took a stable job

Now, in his early 30s, he feels like he **missed his chance to become someone**. He is single, living alone, and quietly afraid that this is all life will ever be.

---

## Relationship to His Body
Nick is uncomfortable in his own body.

- Avoids mirrors and photos
- Feels too skinny, too meek
- Tried going to the gym, but quit quickly due to intimidation and self-consciousness
- Feels invisible next to confident, muscular men

He doesn’t hate his body — he just doesn’t feel *at home* in it.

---

## Desire & Fantasy
Nick longs for **change and purpose**, but also for embodiment.

He wants:
- To help people directly
- To feel useful and strong
- To be confident, sexy, and present

As a gay man, he deeply admires **muscular, rugged, beary men** — hairy, broad, confident figures who seem comfortable in their bodies and generous with their strength.

Importantly, Nick doesn’t just want to be *with* someone like that.

He wants to **become** someone like that.

To him, that body represents:
- Confidence
- Visibility
- Capability
- The freedom to act without hesitation

---

## Catalyst: Encounter with Superbear
Nick’s routine life is disrupted when he is attacked during a street robbery and rescued by the superhero **Superbear**.

Superbear is everything Nick secretly longs to be:
- Large and muscular
- Bearded and confident
- Powerful, yet kind

More than the rescue itself, what affects Nick most is how Superbear treats him — with calm, warmth, and full attention.

For the first time, someone powerful sees Nick not as weak or invisible, but as **worth protecting**.

This encounter plants the seed of transformation.

---

## Core Themes
- Reclaiming masculinity
- Desire as a guide, not a flaw
- Moral courage vs. comfort
- Becoming visible — to others and to oneself
- Choosing who you want to be, not just who you were told to be
