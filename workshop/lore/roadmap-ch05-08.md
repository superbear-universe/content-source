# CHAPTER 5 — _The Corruption_

### _(Nick POV · Finished)_

### Narrative Purpose

- Show Nick being emotionally sidelined without cruelty
- Establish the cost of Mike’s distance
- Introduce corrupted bearing as seductive and wrong
- End with Nick’s removal from the board

**Core idea:** When intimacy stalls, clarity is sought elsewhere.

---

### Emotional Arc

Warm → Hopeful → Confused → Rejected → Seeking → Unsettled → Gone

---

### Locked Beats (from `ch5-the-corruption.md`)

#### 1. Domestic intimacy without completion

- Nick and Mike share a comfortable, affectionate domestic space
- Physical closeness exists but consistently halts before escalation
- Mike deflects intimacy with humor and gentleness
- Nick feels cared for but _unmet_

This is not neglect — it is hesitation.

---

#### 2. The moment of rejection

- A specific, quiet moment where Nick reaches emotionally or physically
- Mike pulls back, subtly but decisively
- No argument, no accusation
- The absence of explanation hurts more than refusal would

Nick internalizes the rejection.

---

#### 3. Seeking relief, not rebellion

- Nick goes out alone
- Motivation is not cheating or self-destruction
- He wants sensation, noise, and to feel wanted without pause

This choice is framed as understandable.

---

#### 4. The corrupted bearing

- Nick witnesses a corrupted bearing in a semi-private club space
- Voluntary submission, harnesses, calm relief
- No violence, no panic — only narrowing
- The process feels _finished_, not expansive

Nick does not fully understand what he’s seeing — only that it is wrong.

---

#### 5. Capture

- Nick is noticed observing
- He is contained efficiently
- A single, clean blow
- Immediate loss of consciousness

---

### Ending State

Nick is unconscious.

Not punished.  
Not warned.

Simply removed.

---

# CHAPTER 6 — _Protective Bear Instinct_

### _(Mike POV · Finished)_

### Narrative Purpose

- Reframe Mike’s distance as fear-driven
- Show Superbear acting from panic, not confidence
- Force the cost of secrecy into the open
- Establish that protection-through-avoidance fails

**Core idea:** Fear makes decisions feel urgent — and wrong ones easier.

---

### Emotional Arc

Alarm → Focus → Dread → Rage → Exposure → Guilt → Resolve

---

### Locked Beats

#### 1. Absence triggers panic

- Mike realizes Nick is missing
- Timing and silence escalate fear rapidly
- Superbear emerges from panic, not heroism

---

#### 2. Tracking and intrusion

- Mike tracks Nick to Butch Bear’s territory
- The space feels ideologically hostile
- Order, hierarchy, and ownership are palpable

Mike is already off-balance.

---

#### 3. Psychological confrontation

- Butch Bear frames Mike’s behavior accurately:

    - Protection through distance
    - Deciding instead of trusting
- These statements are not taunts — they land because they’re true
    

---

#### 4. Violence without joy

- Superbear uses force decisively and aggressively
    
- No banter, no playfulness
    
- This is fear-driven urgency
    

---

#### 5. Extraction

- Mike rescues Nick while he is semi-conscious
    
- He refuses to leave him unattended
    
- Priority is Nick, not victory
    

---

#### 6. The identity fracture

- Mike brings Nick to safety — Nick’s home
    
- Superbear should not know this location
    
- Nick notices familiarity and tone even through disorientation
    

Recognition begins without full clarity.

---

### Ending State

Nick is safe.

Mike’s secrecy is no longer intact.
