## Superbear Chapter 4.5:
# A bear hug of belonging

Derek had learned, over time, how to move through places without asking anything of them.

It was a skill, really. The way he kept his shoulders slightly rounded, his steps measured, his presence tidy. Not shrinking exactly—just leaving room. For younger men. Louder men. Men who looked like they already belonged.

The bar wasn’t unfriendly. It never was. It was warm, busy in a low-key way, the kind of place that didn’t announce itself as anything in particular. Wood, amber light, bodies leaning close enough to be comfortable. Derek had picked it on purpose. Neutral ground. Safe.

He sat with his beer and let himself watch.

He’d been doing that for decades, if he was honest. Watching without intruding. Learning the shapes of things he wasn’t sure he was allowed to want.

The bears—he didn’t think the word loudly, but it was there, warm and familiar. Big men with easy laughter. Thick arms draped over chair backs. Beards that looked earned rather than curated. They took up space without apology, without checking first. They stayed.

That was the part that always caught in his chest.

Not the size. Not the hair. The staying.

For him, being a bear had never meant dominance or swagger. It meant warmth. Brotherhood. The sense that you could sit somewhere and not have to explain yourself, not have to perform a version of masculinity that kept shifting its rules.

A place where you were allowed to settle.

Derek took a sip of his beer and smiled faintly at nothing in particular.

At forty-six, newly out and newly untethered, he felt late in a way that was hard to articulate. Not late to sex, exactly. Not even late to love. Late to inhabiting himself. Late to choosing anything that hadn’t been chosen for him already.

He glanced down at his clothes—button-up shirt, beige trousers. Respectable. Anonymous. Dressed for a life that had made sense once and now sat on him like borrowed furniture.

No one here was rejecting him. He knew that, intellectually. No one had ever told him he didn’t belong.

That almost made it worse.

It was easier to believe there was some clear rule he had failed to meet than to admit the truth: that he’d been quietly stepping aside long before anyone asked him to.

He finished his drink slowly, feeling the familiar tiredness settle in—not exhaustion exactly, more like a constant low-grade effort. The effort of monitoring himself. Of checking whether he was taking up too much room.

Derek stood, smoothing his shirt out of habit, and headed for the door.

Outside, the night air was cool against his face. He paused on the sidewalk, hands slipping into his pockets, and felt the familiar question rise up—not _what do I want?_ but _am I allowed to want it?_

The answer, as always, hovered just out of reach.

And then, somewhere nearby, metal screamed against metal.

The sound cut through the night like a mistake.

Metal shrieked, sharp and immediate, followed by a heavy, ugly _thump_ that Derek felt in his chest more than heard. He flinched, head snapping toward the intersection just in time to see a car skid sideways and slam into a parked van.

For a second, everything went very still.

Steam—or smoke, he couldn’t tell yet—curled up from the crumpled hood. One headlight flickered, then went out. The engine made a thin, unhappy whine.

“Oh—” Derek said, already moving.

He crossed the street without thinking, shoes scuffing against the pavement as his brain caught up in fragments. _Car accident. Driver. Call someone._ His hand was already digging his phone out of his pocket.

The driver’s door was crushed inward, jammed tight. Derek leaned toward the shattered window, heart thudding.

“Hey,” he called, louder than he meant to. “Hey—are you okay?”

The man inside didn’t move.

Derek swallowed. He fumbled his phone awake, thumb sliding once, twice, missing the screen.

“Okay. Okay,” he muttered, not sure who he was saying it to.

He dialed emergency services, voice pitching too high as he gave the address, eyes never leaving the car. He crouched, peering in through the broken glass. The driver’s head was slumped forward at an unnatural angle, chest barely moving.

“Sir?” Derek tried again. “Can you hear me?”

Nothing.

He reached out instinctively, then stopped.

The door wouldn’t open. The metal frame was bent, compressed in on itself. Derek braced his feet and put his hands against it anyway, pushing experimentally.

It didn’t budge.

He pushed harder, shoulders tightening, breath catching as he leaned his weight into it. The door shuddered, then settled back into place, unmoved.

A hot, frustrated sound escaped his throat.

“Come on,” he whispered. “Come on.”

This was the moment, wasn’t it? The one where you were supposed to _do_ something. Pull someone free. Break a window. Take charge.

Derek’s hands slid uselessly along the dented metal. He knew, intellectually, that most people couldn’t wrench open a crushed car door. He knew emergency responders had tools for this.

That didn’t stop the shame from blooming anyway.

He stood there, phone still pressed to his ear, listening to the dispatcher’s calm instructions while his body lagged half a step behind his intent. Every movement felt slightly delayed, like he was trying to operate himself through water.

“I’m here,” he said into the phone. “I’m right here with him.”

A couple of other bystanders had gathered now, hovering at a careful distance. One of them asked if help was coming. Derek nodded too quickly.

“Yes. Yes, they’re on their way.”

He knelt again, closer this time, forcing himself to stay present. He could do that, at least. Stay.

“Help’s coming,” he told the unconscious driver, voice softer now. “You’re not alone.”

His reflection wavered in the cracked glass—narrow face, thinning hair, eyes too wide. He looked like someone _trying_. Someone who meant well.

Someone who wasn’t enough.

The thought landed with a dull, familiar weight. Not panic. Not fear. Just that old, grinding frustration. The sense of being outpaced by the moment. Of arriving emotionally before his body could catch up.

Sirens sounded in the distance, faint but unmistakable.

Derek exhaled, shoulders sagging with relief that felt undeserved. He stayed where he was until the flashing lights painted the street red and blue, until professionals took over, voices firm and practiced.

Only then did he step back, hands hanging uselessly at his sides, heart still racing.

He hadn’t run.

But he hadn’t really _helped_ either.

And the difference sat heavy in his chest as the night closed in around the scene.

The sirens grew louder, then cut off abruptly.

For a moment Derek thought the emergency vehicles had arrived faster than expected. He turned toward the sound—and then stopped.

Something _else_ had entered the street.

There was no explosion, no dramatic landing. Just a sudden, undeniable shift in the air, like the moment a room goes quiet without anyone asking for it.

Superbear stood near the wrecked car.

He was bigger than Derek had expected. Not just tall or muscular, but solid in a way that read as settled rather than aggressive. Broad shoulders, thick arms, a weight to him that felt intentional. His suit caught the streetlights in muted blues and reds, gold trim glinting softly instead of shouting.

He didn’t rush.

He assessed the scene with a quick, practiced glance—smoke, metal, bystanders, the unconscious driver—and moved.

Derek watched as Superbear crouched beside the car, hands bracing against the crushed door. There was no strain in his posture. No preparatory flexing. He simply _applied himself_.

The metal screamed as it gave way.

Not explosively. Not violently. It peeled back under Superbear’s grip like something finally agreeing to stop resisting. He tore the door free and set it aside with careful precision, as if it weighed no more than a thought.

Derek’s breath caught.

Superbear leaned into the car, one massive arm sliding behind the driver’s shoulders, the other supporting his legs. He lifted him out in a single, smooth motion, cradling the man close to his chest as if this were a familiar, rehearsed act.

Which, Derek realized, it probably was.

“Easy,” Superbear murmured—not to the crowd, but to the unconscious man himself. His voice carried just far enough to be heard. Calm. Grounded. Certain.

Derek felt something inside him loosen as the driver was laid gently on the pavement, Superbear already checking breathing, positioning him with efficient care. There was no hesitation, no wasted movement.

This wasn’t bravery.

This was alignment.

_Oh,_ Derek thought distantly. _This is what it looks like when someone can just… do this._

Firefighters and paramedics arrived seconds later, spilling into the space Superbear had already cleared for them. He stepped back immediately, ceding the scene without comment, answering a few clipped questions before they turned their full attention to the driver.

No one applauded. No one needed to.

Superbear stood to the side now, cape settling behind him, scanning the bystanders with the same steady focus he’d given the wreck.

His gaze passed over Derek without stopping.

And that, strangely, made the moment land harder.

Derek watched him from a few steps back, heart still thudding, hands still useless at his sides. He felt small—not in comparison, exactly, but in contrast. Like two versions of being present had briefly occupied the same space.

One capable of acting without friction.

One trying very hard to keep up.

As the professionals worked and the street slowly returned to motion, Derek stayed where he was, unable to look away.

Superbear hadn’t come _for_ him.

But Derek couldn’t shake the feeling that he had just seen something important.

- - -

Derek didn’t know how long he sat there.

Long enough for the noise to change — sirens fading into distance, voices thinning out, the crowd reorganizing itself into smaller clusters of bystanders and helpers. Long enough for his hands to start aching from where they were knotted in his hair, fingers pressing against his scalp like he could hold the moment still if he tried hard enough.

He forced himself to let go.

The bench was cold through his clothes, metal biting faintly into the backs of his thighs. Across the street, the wrecked car looked smaller now, less immediate. Controlled. Handled. People in reflective jackets moved with purpose, and the man who had been trapped inside it was gone — lifted away on a stretcher, wrapped in blankets, alive.

Derek knew that should be enough.

Instead, all he could feel was the hollow space where his usefulness should have been.

He’d frozen. Stood there with his phone in his hand, heart hammering, brain locked in place while other people acted. While Superbear had acted. Derek had wanted to help — had felt that desperate, clawing need to do something — but his body hadn’t listened. It never did, when it mattered.

He bent forward, elbows braced on his knees, head dropping into his hands.

Stupid. Useless. Too slow.

A shadow fell across the pavement in front of him.

Derek tensed, then forced himself not to flinch as the bench dipped slightly under new weight. The presence beside him was… large. Solid. Close enough that Derek could feel warmth through the winter air.

“You mind if I sit?” a calm voice asked.

Derek looked up.

Superbear stood close now. Up close, he looked less like a symbol and more like a person wearing one — broad shoulders filling out the suit, the smooth blue-and-red fabric stretched cleanly across his chest. His beard framed a face that wasn’t stern or heroic. Just attentive. Grounded.

Derek swallowed. “No. I mean— it’s fine.”

They sat in silence for a moment. Not an awkward one. The kind that waits to see if it’s needed.

“I saw you over here,” Superbear said eventually. “Figured you might not be ready to head home yet.”

Derek huffed out something that might’ve been a laugh if it had more air in it. “That obvious?”

“Only if you know what to look for.”

Derek stared down at his hands. They were still trembling, just a little. He clenched them into fists and then loosened them again, as if testing whether they still belonged to him.

“I tried to help,” he said, quietly. “At the car. I really did.”

Superbear didn’t interrupt.

“I didn’t know what to do. I wanted to do something. But I just— froze. Everyone else moved. You moved.” His throat tightened. “I stood there like an idiot with my phone out, thinking, this is it, this is the moment you finally matter, and I still couldn’t.”

Superbear nodded once, slow and deliberate. “Freezing doesn’t mean you didn’t care.”

“It felt like failure,” Derek said. The word came out sharper than he’d intended. He winced, then sighed. “It always does.”

He rubbed his face, dragging his palms down over his eyes. When he dropped them, he realized he was shaking again — not from the cold, but from everything finally catching up.

“I wasn’t even having a good night before that,” he added, almost bitterly. “This was supposed to be… I don’t know. A win. Something normal.”

Superbear tilted his head slightly. “You want to tell me about it?”

Derek hesitated. This was ridiculous. He didn’t know this man. Not really. And yet— the way Superbear sat there, unhurried, not looming despite his size, made it feel strangely safe to keep talking.

“I went to a bar,” Derek said. “A gay bar. Thought maybe if I just… showed up enough times, it would start to feel like somewhere I belonged.”

“And it didn’t.”

“No.” Derek’s mouth twisted. “I stuck out. Too old. Too awkward. Everyone else looked like they knew the rules already. I kept thinking if I just tried harder, if I smiled more, stood differently, became someone else—”

He shook his head. “I left early.”

Superbear was quiet for a beat. Then: “That doesn’t sound like a failure. That sounds like someone trying.”

Derek let out a short, humorless breath. “You don’t know the half of it.”

He stared out at the street, at the place where everything had gone wrong and then right without him. “I came out late. Lost my wife. Lost my family. Or—” He shrugged weakly. “They left. Same difference. Everyone says it gets better, but sometimes it just feels like I traded one kind of loneliness for another.”

Superbear’s gaze stayed on the street too, as if giving Derek space rather than scrutiny.

“I see these guys,” Derek continued. “Bears. Big, warm, loud. They look like they belong to each other. Like there’s room for them. And I want that so badly it hurts.” His voice dropped. “But I don’t think I’m built for it. Not really. I don’t think I’m… enough.”

For the first time, Superbear turned fully toward him.

“You know,” he said gently, “most people who look like they belong somewhere once felt exactly like you do.”

Derek frowned. “You’re just being nice.”

“I’m being honest,” Superbear replied. “Belonging isn’t something you earn by fitting a shape. It’s something that happens when you’re allowed to take up space.”

Derek looked at him then — really looked. At the size of him. The calm certainty. The way his presence didn’t push, didn’t crowd.

“I don’t know how to do that,” Derek admitted.

Superbear’s expression softened, something warm settling into his eyes. “Would you want that to be different?”

Superbear nodded once.

“I can help with that,” he said.

Derek looked up, startled.

“Not by teaching you,” Superbear added. “And not slowly. I mean your body. I can help it change.”

He let that sit for a moment.

“You’d get bigger. Stronger. A lot more beary. It is irreversible though.”

He didn’t smile. Didn’t soften it.

“It’s not for everyone. But if that’s what you want — it’s possible.”

Derek didn’t answer right away.

He looked down at his hands. At the way his fingers were folded together, knuckles pale from holding too tight. A lifetime of reflexes surfaced all at once — the instinct to minimize, to step back, to ask whether he was allowed.

His mouth opened. Closed again.

“I don’t—” He stopped, exhaled. “I don’t know if I get to just… choose something like that.”

Superbear didn’t interrupt. Didn’t fill the space.

Derek swallowed. The question that always came first rose to the surface, familiar and tired.

“Why me?”

The silence held.

Derek straightened slightly. Not all the way — just enough to feel it.

“I keep waiting for someone to tell me I qualify,” he said quietly. “And it never happens.”

He shook his head once, almost amused.

“I’m tired of waiting.”

He looked up.

“Yes,” Derek said. This time there was no wobble in it. “I want this.”

Superbear nodded once. Then got up from the bench.

“Alright,” Superbear said. “Come here.”

Derek rose from the bench, legs still a little unsteady, and stepped closer.

Superbear’s hand came to rest flat against Derek’s chest.

The contact was warm. Solid. Not pressing — just there, anchoring him. Derek became acutely aware of the size of that hand, the steady weight of it through the thin cotton of his shirt.

For a breath, nothing happened.

Then a glow bloomed beneath Superbear’s palm.

Soft at first — a warm, rainbow wash of color that seeped through fabric and skin alike, spreading outward in slow, overlapping bands. Derek felt it before he really saw it: a gentle heat, reassuring, almost pleasant, as if something was testing the shape of him.

He exhaled.

And then it goes wrong.

Too fast — too sharp.

The warmth spikes, surging hard through his shoulders and back like something forcing itself under his skin. Derek gasps, breath stalling as pressure slams into him from both sides, driving outward with nowhere to go.

“No—”
The word breaks apart in his throat.

His shoulders pull wider, muscle packing on faster than skin can follow. The burn is immediate — deep, tearing — and his button-up jerks tight across his back, collar biting into his throat as his chest swells beneath it.

Pain flares bright enough to steal his vision.

“Oh—oh, wait—” He staggers, heart hammering wildly. “This—this hurts—”

His chest expands violently with a breath he didn’t choose, ribs protesting as they shove outward. Buttons dig into his sternum, fabric cutting into skin stretched too tight over a body that’s growing wrong, unnaturally fast.

Derek makes a sound somewhere between a gasp and a whimper.

“I— I can’t—”

Hands catch his arms before he falls.

Solid. Massive. Warm.

Superbear is suddenly there, close enough that Derek can feel the heat of him, the sheer presence of another body bracing his from behind.

“Hey,” Superbear says, low and immediate. Not calm — focused. “I’ve got you.”

The pressure spikes again, and Derek cries out as his spine straightens hard, vertebrae shifting into alignment with a sharp, disorienting jolt of pain.

“I didn’t know—” Derek pants, panic spiraling. “I didn’t know it would—”

“I know,” Superbear says, arms firm around his upper torso now, holding him steady as his body fights itself. “You’re not doing anything wrong. Breathe with me.”

Derek tries. Fails.

The pain rolls downward, crushing through his midsection as mass slams into him, skin screaming tight as his center thickens all at once. His stomach forces forward, weight arriving faster than balance can adjust.

He claws at his shirt, desperate.

“It’s too tight—”

Superbear’s hands guide his, quick and sure. “Yeah. Get it off.”

Derek fumbles at the buttons, fingers thickening even as he works them loose. He shrugs the button-up off his shoulders with a broken sob as cool air hits overheated skin.

For half a second, relief—

Then the v-neck stretches viciously across his chest as it keeps growing, fabric biting into skin already burning.

“No—” Derek chokes. “Please—make it stop—”

Superbear shifts behind him, stance widening, grounding them both. One arm braces Derek across the chest, the other solid at his waist, holding him together as his hips spread suddenly, violently, balance dropping out from under him.

“I’ve got you,” Superbear repeats, voice firm now, close to Derek’s ear. “Let it happen. Don’t fight it.”

“I can’t— it hurts—”

“I know. Stay with me.”

Derek’s thighs thicken with grinding pressure, legs shaking as his pants tear at the seams. He cries out again, sharp and raw, hands gripping at Superbear’s forearm like it’s the only solid thing left.

Then—

The pressure changes.

The pain crests — spikes — and suddenly breaks.

Not gradually.

Abruptly.

The burning ache that’s been tearing through him simply… shuts off.

Derek sobs once, stunned, breath stuttering as his body goes slack against Superbear’s chest.

“Oh—”

The pressure that remains is different now.

Not forcing outward.

But closing in.

Superbear’s arms tighten instinctively, pulling Derek fully against him — broad chest at his back, solid forearms across his ribs and waist — a full, enclosing bear hug that braces him from every side.

“Yeah,” Superbear murmurs, feeling the shift. “That’s it.”

Warmth floods in where pain used to be.

Deep. Saturating. Everywhere.

Derek’s breath drops all at once, a long, shaking exhale leaving him as his body settles into the hold instead of fighting it. His skin loosens into its new breadth, stretching comfortably now instead of burning.

His body keeps changing — but now it feels right.

Heat ripples gently across his chest, and this time it makes him shiver in a completely different way.

Hair begins to grow.

Slowly.

Pleasantly.

Coarse warmth spreads beneath the stretched neckline of the v-neck, dark strands brushing against skin and fabric alike. Derek lets out a breathless laugh, surprised by how good it feels — grounding, comforting, like being covered instead of exposed.

“Oh,” he breathes, amazed. “That feels—”

“Yeah,” Superbear says quietly, unmistakable pride in his voice.

The sensation moves down his arms, hair filling in where his skin has always felt too bare. His jaw tingles — not painfully now, but richly — as his beard grows in, dense and warm beneath his fingertips when he reaches up to touch it.

He laughs again, this time openly.

“Oh my—”

Something loosens at the crown of his head. Derek barely notices it at first — just a release, a lightness — until he brushes his hand back and feels smooth, warm skin instead of thinning hair.

There’s no shock.

No loss.

Just relief so clean it makes him grin.

The change finishes through his legs, weight arriving with calm authority as his stance widens naturally, boots planted solidly on the pavement. The v-neck finally tears at the shoulder as his arms broaden past it, but there’s no pain — just sensation, cotton brushing against furred skin with every deep breath he takes.

Superbear loosens his hold slightly, hands still there, ready.

Derek straightens slowly, experimentally.

His body answers without hesitation.

He laughs, breathless and disbelieving, excitement sparking through him bright and electric.

“Oh,” he says, eyes shining now. “Oh—I like this.”

Superbear chuckles softly behind him. “Yeah. I figured you might.”

Derek looks down at himself — the breadth of his chest, the thickness of his arms, the weight settled comfortably in his gut — and then lifts his head, grin spreading wide.

The fear is gone.
The pain is gone.

What’s left feels good.
Better than good.

He hasn’t just made it through.

He’s excited to see what he’s becoming — and for the first time, his body is excited too.

For a moment, Derek just stands there, breathing.

Not to steady himself — he doesn’t need to — but because it feels good. The night air is cool against his skin, against hair that’s still new enough to register as sensation, and every breath fills him completely. There’s no lag anymore. No catching up.

He looks at Superbear, then down at himself, then back again, and laughs — a bright, incredulous sound that slips out before he can stop it.

“I—” He shakes his head, grinning openly now. “I don’t even know what to say.”

Superbear smiles at him, easy and proud, like he’s watching something land exactly where it’s meant to. “You don’t have to.”

Derek swallows, emotion rising up fast and warm in his chest. “No. I do.” He meets Superbear’s gaze, eyes shining. “Thank you. Really. I’ve… I’ve spent a long time feeling like I was late to my own life.”

He gestures vaguely at himself, at the space he now occupies without effort. “This feels like I finally caught up.”

Superbear nods once, quiet and sincere. “You did the hard part.”

Something in Derek’s chest tightens — not with fear, but with a sudden, bubbling joy that needs somewhere to go. He steps forward without thinking, arms lifting a little awkwardly, unsure for half a second how to hug someone this big.

Then Superbear laughs softly and closes the distance for him.

The hug is everything Derek hoped it would be.

Broad arms wrap around him, solid and warm, pulling him in against a chest that feels like it could hold the world steady if it needed to. Derek lets out a surprised, giddy sound as he’s gathered up, feet shifting as his body registers what it’s like to be held like this — fully, without reserve.

“Oh,” he laughs, breathless. “Okay. Yeah. I get it now.”

Superbear squeezes once, friendly and firm, then lets him go.

Superbear steps back, giving Derek one last, appraising look — not evaluative, just fond.

“Well,” he says, smiling, “looks like you’ve found your footing.”

Derek snorts softly. “Feels that way.”

Superbear’s smile turns a little more playful. “Good. Then go enjoy it.” He tips his head toward the bar. “No need to rush. Just… take it at your own bear-ings.”

Derek blinks — then breaks into a laugh, sudden and bright. “Wow.”

Superbear grins, unapologetic. “Couldn’t resist.”

He takes a step back, rainbow light already beginning to gather around him. “Take care of yourself, Derek.”

“Yeah,” Derek says, still smiling. “I will.”

Superbear lifts off a moment later, rising smoothly into the night, leaving only a fading streak of color behind him.

Derek watches until he’s gone.

Only then does he turn back toward the bar.

The door is still open, light spilling out onto the pavement, voices and laughter drifting into the night. Derek pauses for a heartbeat out of old habit — then realizes he doesn’t need to.

He walks in.

Heads turn. Not in surprise — in recognition. Someone smiles. Someone lifts a glass in greeting. The space opens around him naturally, like it’s been waiting.

Derek feels it immediately.

The ease.
The warmth.
The simple, unquestioned sense of being allowed to stay.

The door swings shut behind him.

And for the first time in his life, Derek doesn’t disappear into the room.

He belongs there.
