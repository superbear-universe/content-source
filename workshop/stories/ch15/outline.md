## POV & Tense

Close third person, past tense. Consistent with the Andrew standalone (*Remaking Andrew*) and the main series chapters.

## Setting

**Naples, Italy.** Andrew has deliberately put distance between himself and home (Bologna) because he cannot explain to his family what happened to his body. Naples was chosen for distance and for its geography — a major port city with organised crime infrastructure, which makes sense for a crime fighter operating independently.

**Specific locations:**

- Andrew’s apartment — small, anonymous, functional. Background texture only — not a scene in this chapter.
- Piazza Bellini — his regular spot. A café on the piazza where he takes his daily espresso and reads. This is where the chapter’s civilian scenes are anchored.
- The café interior and back room — the site of the Brotherhood encounter.

-----

## Timeframe

A few months after the events of the Andrew standalone. He has been in Naples long enough to have routines, a regular café, a freelance income. Not long enough to feel fully settled. The body has been adapted to physically — he no longer knocks things over or miscalculates doorframes — but the identity adjustment is still ongoing.

-----

## Andrew’s Civilian Life (Established)

- **Living situation:** Small apartment in Naples. Chosen for distance from family, not for attachment to the city.
- **Income:** Freelance translation work — international law and political documents. Uses his abandoned degree’s skills practically. Flexible hours, done mostly remotely. Pays the bills without requiring him to commit to a career.
- **Future plans:** Vague. He intends to return to studying eventually. That decision remains deferred.
- **Family:** His family believes he is still studying abroad. This is not a plot point — background texture only.
- **Daily routine:** Espresso and reading on Piazza Bellini, typically mid-morning between translation jobs. He is reading *Il Principe* by Machiavelli — a detail that is not commented on directly but carries thematic resonance. A former political science student reading about power and governance while unknowingly sitting above a Brotherhood operation.
- **Super Orso activity:** Operating in Naples for a few months. He has found a rhythm — he approaches fighting with competence and without fear. The city has needed him.

-----

## Andrew’s Emotional State

He is in a better place than he was. The recovery from Brotherhood corruption was hard and slow; what came after — becoming Super Orso — reoriented everything. He does not regret what he is now. If he is honest with himself, he actually likes being big, muscular, and hairy. That acknowledgement is quiet and a little reluctant, like something he has arrived at without intending to.

The remaining dissonance is identity rather than body. He is twenty years old, living alone in a city he chose for negative reasons, doing freelance work as a placeholder, operating as a superhero without a team or a framework. The question of what his life is supposed to look like has not been answered. He has stopped expecting it to answer itself.

He is functional. He is not unhappy. He is unresolved.

## Chapter Register

**The opening (Act 1):** Warm and sensual in register. Andrew on the piazza, physically at ease in his body, comfortable in his routine. The body appreciation beat is quiet and unhurried — never performed, always felt. Roberto present from the start.

**The café encounter (Act 2):** Grounded and observational. Andrew reads the room before he acts. The tonal shift from ordinary morning to something wrong should be gradual, built through behaviour and body language rather than declared.

**The fight:** Demanding. The goons are goo-corrupted and physically enhanced — three of them in a tight space is a genuine challenge even for Andrew. He takes hits. He works for the win. The fight shows on him when he emerges. He wins because he is better, not because they are easy.

**The aftermath and text (Act 3):** Quieter. The incomplete picture of what he found. Then the text, and the chapter ending on his reading it.

## Act Structure

### Act 1 — Opening / Café

The chapter opens directly on Piazza Bellini. Andrew already at his table — espresso, *Il Principe*, Naples morning around him. No mirror scene. The apartment and morning routine exist as background texture only, referenced briefly if at all.

**The body appreciation beat.** Warm, sensual, unhurried — but never performed. Three distinct moments woven across the opening page:

1. **The breeze.** A light wind moves across the piazza and through his open collar. He feels it in his chest hair — something he did not have before. Ambient and involuntary. Naples doing something to him rather than him doing anything for anyone.
1. **The bicep.** He lifts the espresso cup. His sleeve pulls back. He catches a glimpse of his own forearm and upper arm and holds on it for a half-second before drinking. No internal comment. The pause is the beat.
1. **The pec touch.** He is reading — genuinely absorbed — and his free hand drifts to his chest, fingers settling into the hair there. He doesn’t notice he’s done it until a moment later. Reflex, not intention. He doesn’t move the hand away.

The register is quiet appreciation, not vanity. He is comfortable in his body in a way that still occasionally surprises him.

**New reader grounding.** A single line in Act 1 — woven naturally into the opening, not announced — establishes that Andrew has been operating in Naples for a few months as Super Orso. This is sufficient context for readers arriving without the standalone. No further backstory summary is needed.

**Roberto.** He is present from the opening. The greeting between them is easy and established — two people who know each other’s rhythms. Roberto brings the espresso without being asked.

**Roberto’s pressure.** The reader needs one grounded reason to understand why Roberto said yes — not a full backstory, just enough to forgive him. A prior threat, evidence of what happened to someone who refused, debt, family vulnerability. This does not need to be stated directly — it can be implied through a single detail Roberto lets slip in the Act 3 scene, or through something Andrew observes about the café itself. The decision of which reason is left to prose drafting, but something concrete must be present.

**Seeded detail:** When Roberto brings the espresso, he glances at the back room before he looks at Andrew. A fraction of a second — the kind of thing that doesn’t register consciously. Andrew files it as nothing. Later, when the delivery man goes through that same door, it will recontextualise without needing to be underlined.

### Act 2 — The Café Encounter

**The tipping point.** Andrew is at his usual table when something shifts. A delivery man arrives with boxes — except he is wrong for the role. Wrong build, wrong clothes, wrong energy. Not the kind of man who delivers things for a living. Roberto’s reaction to him confirms it: not the wariness of a man being extorted, but the specific contracted stillness of someone who has been told to expect this and told not to react. Andrew watches without appearing to watch. The delivery man goes through to the back room. He is one of the three men Andrew will shortly fight.

**The recognition.** Something moves through Andrew before his brain has assembled a reason for it — a faint sense of wrong energy, familiar in a way he doesn’t want to examine. He was inside that energy once. It ran through him and held him and he knows its frequency even now, even at this distance, even without being able to name what he’s feeling. The violet eyes are the first visible confirmation — wrong before anything else registers, the colour of something corrupted rather than chosen. The rubber accessory is the second, more analytical beat. By the time his brain catches up, his body already knew.

**The weight of routine.** He has been coming to this café for months. The thought that this has been here, inside his ordinary routine, lands before he can stop it.

**He acts.** He leaves the café. Transformation is quick — he has done this enough times that the process is automatic, bear energy gathered and released in one concentrated movement. [*Framework shorthand only — “bear energy” cannot appear in published prose.*] He is back inside in under a minute, and he is no longer Andrew.

**The fight.** Three Brotherhood men in a back room about the size of a domestic garage — tight, cluttered with crates, no good angles for anyone. The goons are goo-corrupted and physically enhanced beyond normal human capability — this is not a clean or easy fight. The space works against Andrew as much as the men do.

Andrew’s combat style is strategic and construct-based — blue geometric forms, shields, gladii, maces — but the tight space limits what he can deploy. No sweeping attacks. He uses a shield construct to pin one man against shelving. He uses the crates and walls as tactical constraints rather than obstacles. He takes a hit because he misreads the goo-corrupted men’s strength — a recalibration moment that costs him something and shows on him afterward. He adapts. He wins by control, not raw force.

The fight should feel like Andrew specifically — a man who reads opponents and environments rather than one who simply hits harder. Roberto is somewhere in the building — afraid, not harmed. The fight should show on Andrew when he emerges.

**The aftermath — Brotherhood materials.** After the fight, Andrew finds crates in the back room containing harnesses, wristbands, and — most prominently — sealed containers of a black viscous substance. He does not know what it is. He was corrupted through cigar smoke and kept in place by a harness; this material is unfamiliar to him intellectually. But his body responds to it before his mind does. Something in the containers calls to him at a frequency he thought he had left behind — not tempting exactly, but insistent. A dark pull, specific and wrong.

He takes one container with him. It is sealed in thick dark glass, small enough to pocket but heavy for its size. He wraps it before pocketing it. He knows this is reckless. He does it anyway — because evidence matters, and leaving it here feels worse than taking it. Andrew is analytical, not careless: the chapter should make clear that he understands the risk and makes a deliberate choice.

### Act 3 — Aftermath / The Text

Andrew back in civilian clothes. Roberto can’t quite meet his eyes — the shame of a man who said yes once because he had no choice and has been living with it since. He starts to say something and stops. Then gives Andrew the one thing he’s been holding: the deliveries didn’t only come here. He overheard something — not meant for him, not much — but enough to understand that whatever came through his back room was going to other places. Not just Naples. Not just Italy. He doesn’t know more than that. He wasn’t supposed to know even that much.

Andrew doesn’t offer comfort. He doesn’t have any that would hold. He steps back out onto the piazza and starts walking home.

The afternoon has the ordinary texture of Naples in sunshine. The incomplete picture sits with him — what he found, what it means, why the contents of that container felt like something already inside him. The container is in his jacket pocket.

He takes out his phone while walking. Sees the notification. Opens the message from Mike.

*I need your help. It’s Nick.*

He reads it. The chapter ends here — on Andrew reading the text, the day suddenly reframed, the shape of what is coming just visible.

## Brotherhood Naples Operation

The Naples Brotherhood operation is more sophisticated than what Andrew encountered in the original city. Better dressed, more integrated into the city’s existing organised crime infrastructure. The Camorra connection is implied but not stated — the operation uses Naples’ existing criminal geography rather than building parallel to it.

**Corruption method:** The Naples goons are not harnessed. They have been corrupted via goo — Bill’s corrupted bearing substance, produced by Bill and distributed to regional operations like Bruno’s. The goo reshapes itself into the corrupted individual’s outfit, integrating with their clothing rather than sitting on top of it. No visible harness. The conversion is internal.

**Visual markers:**

- **Violet eyes** — primary tell. Wrong before anything else registers. Andrew clocks these first.
- **A subtle rubber or latex accessory** — bracelet, collar detail, ring. Small enough to read as fashion in Naples’ style-conscious culture. Wrong enough to register once the eyes have already flagged something.

No harnesses on the goons. Harnesses remain Bill’s personal apparatus and exist in the back room as stored infrastructure — materials for distribution, not worn by these men.

**Bruno (Salvatore Bruno):** Not present in this chapter. Andrew does not know he exists. Bruno does not know Andrew exists. That collision is a later chapter.

## What Andrew Does and Does Not Know at Chapter’s End

**Knows:**

- The Brotherhood has a Naples operation
- The café he has been going to for months was being used as a meeting and storage point
- The materials he found are Brotherhood apparatus — he recognises the harnesses and wristbands from his own corruption
- Something in the black containers is deeply wrong — he can feel it without being able to name it
- He has taken one container with him
- Mike has sent him a message about Nick

**Does not know:**

- Who is running the Naples operation
- What the black substance in the containers actually is or how it is used
- The scale of what is moving through the city
- What has happened to Nick specifically
- What Mike is asking him to do

## Mike’s Text

*I need your help. It’s Nick.*

Sent from the park bench at the end of Chapter 14, after Mike watches corrupted Nick walk Dan into the trees. This is the emotional weight Andrew receives — not an explanation, just six words from a man who does not ask for help easily.

Andrew does not reply within the chapter. His reading of the text is where the chapter ends.

## Thematic Function

Chapter 15 does two things simultaneously:

1. **Establishes Andrew as a fully operational hero with his own city, his own life, and his own ongoing story** — not a supporting character waiting to be called up, but someone with a world that will be pulled toward the main arc rather than simply inserted into it.
1. **Introduces the Brotherhood’s international scale** — the Naples operation shows that what Mike and Nick have been fighting is not local. It is organised, it is distributed, and it has infrastructure in cities Andrew has been calling home. This reframes the rescue arc as something with wider stakes than one city and one corrupted hero.

The Machiavelli detail is thematic texture, not symbolism that needs underlining. A former political science student reading about power and the mechanics of control while sitting above a Brotherhood operation. The reader can do that work.

## Prose Rules (Carry Forward from Project Editorial Standards)

- No “Not X. Y.” negation constructions
- No tricolons
- No staccato fragment chains for emotional emphasis
- No em-dash overuse
- No “the way” simile constructions
- No abstract noun stacking
- No portentous endings or destiny/inevitability language
- No lore vocabulary in published prose (“bear energy,” “bearing”)
- No “Brotherhood” terminology in published prose
- Avoid “sternum” — appears too frequently across stories; find other language for chest area
- Avoid “unhurried” as a repeated descriptor
- Avoid systematic body-sensation lists in transformation sequences — this rule does not apply to the body appreciation beat in Act 1, which is intentionally physical
- Andrew notices systems, posture, and control — not emotions. His observational register is analytical. The recognition beat and the fight should both reflect this: he reads how people stand before he reads what they feel
