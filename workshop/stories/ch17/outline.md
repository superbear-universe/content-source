## Nick's Interior Framing (New — First Nick POV Since Ch11)

This is the first chapter fully inside Nick since the corruption completed. It needs to establish, on the page, what "fully corrupted" actually feels like from the inside — not told, shown.

**The entity.** Kept unnamed or referred to at most as "the entity" — not formalized as a new lore term, not spoken aloud by any character. It is the suit's conditioning loop given interior voice: something pre-verbal, submissive, entirely organized around Bill's approval. It floods warm and eager when Bill is pleased. It curls small and pained when Bill is displeased or corrects him. It has no ambitions of its own beyond that axis. Nick's actual identity — what's left of him, inaccessible to himself — can be referred to in framework notes as "the host," but this is planning shorthand only, not a term for prose.

**Interior voice technique — locked.** The entity gets literal interior thought-lines (italicized), matching the Ch13B precedent (*A SERVE drone — Master will want one of those*). Short, declarative, pre-verbal in register — not eloquent, not reflective. This runs alongside the somatic/physical description rather than replacing it.

**Bill's address for Nick — locked.** Bill calls him "cub." Not his name, not a SERVE-style designation — a pet name that still carries real tenderness, which is its own erasure. Worth landing as a deliberate contrast to SERVE's anonymous three-digit designations without needing to state the contrast outright.

**Nick as general.** An active escalation of his existing "army engine" function. For this operation specifically, Bill has him running real logistics — positioning Boys and Sirs, making calls, giving orders — rather than simply being displayed or pointed at targets. This is both genuine indulgence (Bill showing off what he made) and function (Nick is good at it, and Bill benefits from the competence).

**Full memory, no destabilization.** Nick recognizes people, places, and history accurately. The corruption did not erase his knowledge of who Mike is — it erased what Mike means to him. This should read as more disturbing than amnesia, not less.

**Pride in scale.** Nick registers the scale of this operation as significant, not routine — this is the biggest thing he's been trusted with, and he feels genuine pride at being the one Bill relies on to run it. That pride should be legible on the page, not just inferred by the reader.

## Act Structure

### Act 1 — Bill and Nick, the briefing

A meeting between Bill and Nick going over the operation — not an abstract status update, but something concrete: venue layout, security placement, timing. This is where the master/cub dynamic gets shown rather than stated.

Structure the scene around one correction and one approval:
- **The correction (pain beat):** Bill brings up the Ch13B incident — Nick's unauthorized contact with the SERVE drone — for the first time spoken aloud between them, though Bill has known since it happened. The Seal, as the sustaining force behind Nick's suit, registered the near-failure of the conditioning automatically in the moment it occurred; Bill has been sitting on that knowledge since, and chooses this briefing — right before the biggest operation he's ever trusted Nick with — to finally say it aloud. Framed as both a security lapse and a personal affront (something else nearly took what's his), sharpened by genuine contempt for SERVE's philosophy: their model is anonymous, interchangeable drones absorbed into a hive and reduced to three-digit designations, while Bill's model is singular, personal ownership — Nick as *his*, not inventory. Nick doesn't consciously remember surfacing or thinking of Mike — the suit suppressed that after pulling him back under — so all he has is the shame of having failed Master somehow, without access to why. The reader carries the dramatic irony; Nick doesn't. Physically visible but restrained: audible wincing, a groan he can't quite suppress, face and body tensing hard — not writhing, not collapsing. He stays upright and functional through it, which is its own detail worth landing; the pain is real but it doesn't get to derail him from the conversation. Control persisting through visible suffering is more unsettling than a full breakdown would be.
- **The approval (reward beat):** Nick proposes exhaustive security coverage for the venue — every conventional entrance sealed, funneled, watched, choke points covered by Boys and Sirs. Bill praises the thoroughness, maybe frames it explicitly as proof Nick's learned from the SERVE lapse — closing exactly the kind of vulnerability that let something get close to him before. The flood of relief/warmth this produces should read as close to euphoric, and Nick's body language responds to it visibly (posture, proximity-seeking, the performance of devotion that reads as genuine because to him it is). **Irony for the ending:** neither of them accounts for the roof. The same confidence that earns Nick praise here is exactly what makes the Ch17 crash land — nobody thought to look up.

Physical intimacy here (Bill's hand on his jaw, a kiss, whatever feels earned) reinforces ownership rather than romance — same register as established in Ch11/corrupted character sheet.

No flicker. No crack. This is stable corruption on full display — the most unsettling version of it, precisely because nothing is fighting it from inside.

**Locked: Evan does not appear in this chapter.** The venue callback (same warehouse as Ch5) does the thematic work of tying this event to the Brotherhood's origins without needing him on-page.

### Act 2 — Doors open

The ordinary texture of a Black Ursa crowd arriving — excitement, anticipation, subculture texture — seen entirely through Nick's eyes as he moves through his general duties.

**Revised placement for Mike and Andrew:** not embedded in the crowd. Positioned outside or above — rooftop access, perimeter recon — consistent with a cautious first approach rather than walking straight into hostile territory. Nick has no reason to clock them and doesn't. This preserves the dramatic irony without needing to stage them inside a room Nick is actively managing.

### Act 3 — The Passionate Union

Bill and Nick move into the center of the crowd together. The embrace, the kiss Nick experiences as genuine union, the wave of combined corrupted bearing energy rolling outward. The crowd converts in real time — bodies changing, cries of pain and pleasure indistinguishable, an entire room becoming Bears in seconds, entirely from Nick's satisfied, participating point of view.

**The cut.** As the wave crests or just completes, the roof gives way. Mike and Andrew are visible, breaking in. The chapter ends immediately on this image — the intrusion itself, before anyone in the room has had time to react. No standoff, no dialogue, no resolution. Bill's reaction, Nick's reaction, and everything that follows opens Chapter 18.
