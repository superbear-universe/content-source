# Jacob’s Story

*Draft*

-----

Nick touched down on the outskirts of the city at half past five in the morning, in a retail car park empty enough that no one saw it. He’d gotten better at landings. He still found them slightly absurd.

He switched to civilian clothes — a thought, a flicker of rainbow energy, chinos and a green henley and brown boots where the suit had been — and walked until he found a café that was open. It wasn’t hard. Suburbs always had one place running early for shift workers and insomniacs, and this one had a corner table and reasonable coffee and no one who looked twice at him.

He sat with his mug and thought about the fact that he had flown here. Across the entire country, at altitude, in a few hours, under his own power. That was still a sentence he couldn’t quite make ordinary. Four months ago he’d been taking the bus. Now he was in a café two streets from Jacob’s house having arrived the way weather arrives, and the most remarkable thing about the morning so far was that the coffee was actually good.

He’d known Jacob for four years. Voice calls, D&D sessions that ran until two in the morning, the slow accumulation of someone’s whole interior life exchanged in fragments over time. He knew Jacob’s opinions on every film in the Alien franchise. He knew about the gym phase and the diet that followed and the settled quiet that came after both. He knew the specific rhythm of Jacob’s humour — the way he moved toward self-deprecation before anyone else could get there.

He didn’t know what Jacob looked like standing in a doorway. That was the part that was new.

At eight o’clock he paid, picked up his bag, and walked to Callum Street. The house was the third one — a semi-detached with a blue front door that Jacob had described to him twice in the last week, apparently worried Nick would get it wrong. He stood at it for a moment, settling into it. Then he knocked.

There was a pause — long enough that Nick wondered if he’d gotten the time wrong — and then footsteps, and then the door opened.

Jacob was shorter than Nick had pictured. Broader too, in a soft way, ginger hair pushed flat on one side from sleep, green eyes blinking against the morning light. He looked at Nick for a moment — taking in the henley, the beard, the glasses, the sheer fact of him filling the doorframe — with the specific expression of someone whose brain is buffering.

“You’re,” Jacob said. Then stopped.

“Yeah,” Nick said.

“You’re huge.”

“Little bit.”

Jacob stepped back to let him in, and Nick ducked slightly through the doorway out of habit, and then they were inside. Jacob put the kettle on while Nick looked around — the narrow hallway, the lived-in kitchen, the stairs with a jacket draped over the banister.

“How was your flight?” Jacob called from the kitchen.

“Good,” Nick said. “Fine. Yeah.”

Jacob appeared in the doorway with two mugs. “That’s very specific.”

“It was a very uneventful flight.”

“You’ve been saying for three years you couldn’t afford to come out here.”

“Situation changed,” Nick said.

Jacob looked at him for a moment, decided to let it go, and handed him a mug. “Come on. I’ll show you the room.”

The guestroom was small and clean, with a window that looked out onto the back garden and a slightly apologetic quality that Jacob clearly felt more than the room deserved. He stood in the doorway while Nick set his bag down and pointed out the wardrobe, the lamp, the wifi password written on a Post-it on the desk.

“It’s good,” Nick said, because Jacob was still hovering.

“It’s a bit small.”

“Jacob. It’s fine.”

“The bed’s fine. I checked. My housemate thought I was insane for checking.”

“You checked the bed.”

“I just sat on it. Briefly.”

Nick sat on it. The bed groaned with a conviction that suggested it had never had to work this hard before. “See,” he said.

Jacob relaxed slightly, and they went back downstairs. The kitchen was warm and a little cluttered in the way shared houses always are, and they ended up where people always end up — leaning against the counter with their mugs, talking the way they always talked. His course, a difficult lecturer, the group project he was dreading, a D&D campaign they’d abandoned six months ago and kept meaning to return to.

At some point Jacob turned to put the kettle on again, his back half-turned, and said: “Can I ask you something weird?”

“Sure,” Nick said.

“When did you get so big? Like — built. Beary. All of it. I didn’t know you were a gym person.”

“Just got into it a few years back,” Nick said. He felt bad for lying, but it was easier this way.

“Right.” Jacob turned to face him, leaning back against the counter. “Well. It suits you. Genuinely.” A beat. “I tried the gym thing for a while. Never really took. I just wanted to lose weight, if I’m honest. That was always the whole goal. Get smaller, feel better. Didn’t work, so.” He shrugged. “Stopped trying.”

“Is that still what you want?” Nick said. “To be smaller?”

Jacob considered it. “I want to feel comfortable. I’ve just never been able to picture what that looks like without also being thinner.” He looked up. “Which probably says something.”

“For what it’s worth — I’ve known you for four years. I’ve never once thought about your body. I’ve thought about you — the actual you. That guy has always been enough.”

Jacob looked at him. Something moved across his face that he didn’t quite manage to contain.

“Sorry,” he said. “That’s — “ He exhaled. “No one’s ever actually said that to me before.”

“It’s true,” Nick said simply.

Jacob set his mug down on the counter. He crossed the kitchen and hugged Nick.

The real kind — both arms, full, the kind that means it.

Nick hugged him back. A faint shimmer passed between them, brief as a breath, the colour of something just out of focus. Neither of them saw it.

And then something moved.

It started as warmth — deep under his skin, radiating outward through his ribcage like the first wave of fever heat. Jacob pulled back from the hug instinctively and looked down at his hands.

His fingers were thicker. He could see it happening — the knuckles widening, the tendons rising under the skin — and he could feel it too, a deep internal pressure building up through his palms and into his wrists, his forearms, moving steadily inward and upward like something filling from the inside out.

“Something is happening to me.” He pressed both hands to his chest and felt it there — a low, building heat, his heartbeat stronger than it had been a moment ago, his shirt pulling tight across his shoulders as the muscle beneath it thickened and spread. “Something is actually happening to me right now.”

“Just breathe — “ Nick said.

“Breathe?” Jacob laughed, high and startled. Then he felt it — a tingling across his sternum, needle-fine at first, spreading fast. Under his palms, pushing through the skin in a slow warm wave: hair. He could feel each follicle opening, a thousand tiny points of heat blooming across his chest and down his stomach. He looked down at the gap between his buttons. “Oh my god.”

“Jacob — “

“There’s — there’s actually — “ The words dissolved. The tingling was everywhere now, moving down across his stomach, up over his shoulders and along his forearms, and underneath it the heat kept building, his blood running faster and hotter, his whole body buzzing with it. He pressed his palms flat against his chest and felt the hair thick and coarse and real under his hands and laughed again — differently this time, the panic cracking open into something else entirely.

Then his arms started.

He felt his biceps first — a deep, pressurised bloat of heat moving through the muscle, swelling outward against the fabric of his sleeves, thickening in a long slow surge that made him gasp. He bent his arm instinctively and felt the peak of it press hard against the henley, solid and dense and enormous. His triceps followed, pushing out from the back of his arm in a heavy wave, and he straightened his arm and bent it again just to feel it — the weight of it, the power sitting in it, something he had no previous reference point for.

“My arms,” he said, slightly awed. “Nick, my arms — “

His chest came next — a deep expansion that started at his sternum and moved outward in both directions, the pectoral muscle thickening and pushing forward in two solid slabs, his shirt going taut and then tighter still. He pressed both palms flat against his chest and felt it — broad, solid, real — and the breath went out of him. He pressed harder, feeling the muscle firm beneath his hands, and something moved in him that was purely and simply wonder.

His back widened behind him simultaneously, lats flaring out, shoulders rolling back and down as they filled with mass, his whole upper body restructuring itself around a new centre. His centre of gravity dropped. His feet met the floor differently — heavier, more certain.

Then his belly.

He felt it change last among his torso — a deep internal solidifying, the softness compressing and hardening from the inside out, reshaping itself into something rounder and firmer and more deliberate. He pressed both hands against it and his breath caught. It was solid. A proper gut, dense and round and projecting forward with complete authority, a muscle belly that belonged to a body built to carry it. He pressed his palms into it and felt it push back and laughed out loud — startled and delighted — running his hands over the curve of it, feeling it firm and warm and completely, undeniably his.

“Oh,” he said. Just that. “Oh.”

The heat moved downward. His thighs thickened first — a slow pressurised swelling through the quadriceps, the muscle pushing out against his chinos until the fabric strained, his legs becoming something he could feel with every small shift of weight. His calves followed, rounding out into solid muscle, and when he shifted his stance his legs felt like tree trunks beneath him — wide, planted, built to carry everything that now sat above them. He rocked his weight from foot to foot and felt the floor hold him differently. Felt himself held.

His face came last. His jaw ached pleasantly as it widened and squared, his cheekbones shifting, his whole face rearranging itself into something more defined, more settled. And then the beard came — a rush of tingling across his jaw and chin and upper lip, follicles opening all at once in a warm blooming wave, hair pushing through thick and fast, auburn with copper running through it. He raised both hands to his face and felt it under his palms — dense, full, real — and laughed, full and helpless, not trying to stop it.

The heat ebbed slowly. His heartbeat steadied. The tingling faded to a low warm hum across his skin.

Jacob stood in the kitchen and breathed.

Then he said: “I need to see a mirror.”

He found the one in the hallway and stood in front of it for a long time without speaking.

He was big. Solidly, undeniably big, in a way that read as a fact rather than a condition. His chest was broad and solid beneath his strained shirt. His arms pressed against his sleeves. His belly pushed forward, round and firm, the belly of a man fully at home in his own mass. His legs were planted beneath him like they meant it. He had a beard that looked like it had always been there, auburn with copper running through it, dense and full across his jaw. He looked like someone who had arrived somewhere and intended to stay.

A giggle came out of him. Completely involuntary, slightly unhinged. Then another.

“Jacob?” Nick said from the kitchen doorway.

“I look — “ Jacob started. Stopped. Pressed one hand to his jaw, felt the beard against his palm. Then both hands to his chest. Then down to his belly, pressing into the firm curve of it and shaking his head slowly. “I look like myself,” he said. “I have no idea what that means but that’s what I look like.”

He stood there for a moment longer, then turned away from the mirror to face Nick, who had drifted into the hallway during the exchange and was standing a few feet away. His expression was doing something complicated that Jacob didn’t have the bandwidth to read yet.

“What just happened,” Jacob said. It wasn’t quite a question.

“I — “ Nick stopped.

Jacob looked at him. Then down at himself again. Then back at Nick — the chest, the arms, the beard, the solid comfortable mass of him — and something started assembling itself in the back of his mind that he wasn’t quite ready to look at directly.

“The D&D call,” Jacob said slowly. “Three weeks ago. I could see your face. You were clean shaven. Your shoulders — “ He paused. “Your shoulders weren’t like that.”

Nick said nothing.

“And then you show up today looking like — “ Jacob gestured at Nick, a broad gesture that took in the full situation. “And then we hug. And then this.” He pressed both hands against his chest, felt the solid muscle there, still faintly warm. “Nick.”

Nick looked at him steadily.

“What are you?” Jacob said.

The silence stretched. Nick seemed to be weighing something — how much to say, where to start, whether starting was even the right call right now.

“That’s a longer conversation,” Nick said finally. “Are you okay? Actually okay?”

Jacob considered the deflection. Let it go for now.

“I feel — “ He turned and looked at himself in the mirror again, taking himself in from this angle. His chest rose and fell. His jaw was his jaw, bearded and square, and it was his. “Yeah,” he said. “I’m actually okay.” A beat. “I have about a hundred questions.”

“I know,” Nick said. “I’ll answer them. I promise.”

Jacob turned back to look at him. Something in Nick’s face — the careful steadiness of it, the way he was holding himself — told Jacob that the answers were going to be significant. That whatever Nick was, whatever had just happened, it was bigger than a gym habit that had snowballed.

“You lied to me,” Jacob said. “Earlier. About the gym.”

“Yeah,” Nick said quietly. “I did.”

Jacob looked at him for a long moment. Then he looked down at his hands again — his big, beary, completely transformed hands — and exhaled slowly.

“Okay,” he said. “You’re going to tell me everything.”

“I will,” Nick said. “I promise I will. Just — “ He looked at Jacob, something close to relief moving across his face. “Are you sure you’re okay?”

Jacob pressed his palms against his belly, felt it firm and round beneath his hands, and something settled in him that had been unsettled for a very long time.

“Yeah,” he said. “I really am.”

He crossed the hallway in two strides and threw his arms around Nick.

Nick made a sound of genuine surprise. Jacob was thorough about it — both arms, full, pulling Nick in with a solidity that hadn’t existed an hour ago. He was warm and broad and he held on with the easy confidence of someone who had just discovered that this was something his body could do.

“Thank you,” Jacob said, into Nick’s shoulder. “I don’t know what you did or how you did it and you’re going to explain all of it to me. But thank you.”

Nick laughed despite himself, a short exhale of something that was almost relief. He patted Jacob’s back — which was a broad back now, a bear’s back — and Jacob let him go and stepped back with a grin that took up his whole face.

“Right,” Jacob said. He rolled his shoulders back. Felt them settle. Looked around the kitchen with the expression of someone seeing a familiar room from a new height. “I’m going to make more coffee. You want more coffee?”

“Sure,” Nick said.

Jacob filled the kettle and set it on, moving through the kitchen with an ease that was new — unhurried, comfortable, taking up the space he occupied without apologising for it. He leaned back against the counter while the water heated and crossed his arms over his chest and felt the solidity of them and smiled to himself, private and warm.

They drank the coffee. Jacob asked his questions, slowly, and Nick answered them — carefully, honestly, leaving some things for later but giving Jacob enough to hold. Jacob listened the way he always listened, except that now he did it with his feet planted wide and his big hands wrapped around his mug and an openness in his face that hadn’t quite been there before. The self-deprecation didn’t come. He just listened, open-faced, and let the conversation be what it was.

At some point the afternoon light shifted and Nick picked up his mug and said he might go and lie down for a bit.

“Guestroom’s yours,” Jacob said warmly. “Bed’s been tested. It holds.”

Nick smiled at that and took himself upstairs.

-----

The guestroom was quiet. Nick sat on the edge of the bed and looked at his hands for a while.

He had flown across the country this morning on his own power, which was still a sentence he couldn’t quite make ordinary. He had sat in a café and drunk good coffee and thought about what it meant to be what he was now. He had met his friend in person for the first time and ducked through a doorway and tested a bed and told the truth about something that mattered.

And then he had done something permanent without meaning to. Something that turned out, somehow, to be exactly right.

He sat with that. The relief of Jacob’s face in the mirror. The weight of not having chosen it. The specific complexity of something being both of those things at once.

Outside he could hear Jacob in the kitchen, moving around, the sound of a mug being set down, a quiet laugh at something — nothing, probably, just the particular warmth of someone who has recently discovered they have a laugh worth letting out.

After a while he reached for his phone. Opened a message to Mike. Sat with it for a moment, thumb hovering, then typed:

*turned my first one today. a friend. it was an accident. he’s okay though, really okay. gonna need to talk when I’m back*

He sent it before he could think about it too much. Put the phone face down on the mattress beside him.

Outside, Jacob laughed again. That warm, unguarded laugh.

Nick lay back on the bed. The bed held.

He stared at the ceiling and thought about what kind of person he was now, and what that meant, and how you carried something like today forward into whatever came next.

The answer wasn’t there yet. But for the first time in a while, the question felt like something he could sit with.
