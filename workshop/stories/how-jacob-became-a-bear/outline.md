# Jacob's Story — Outline Document

**Working Title:** TBD
**Type:** Standalone transformation story
**Timeline:** Post-Chapter 9; Nick is established as Supercub. Standalone timeline — does not need to align precisely with main chapter sequence.

---

## Characters

**Jacob** — Mid-twenties, overweight but not beary, ginger hair, green eyes. Barely any body hair, clean-shaven. Carries his body as a problem to be managed rather than a self to inhabit. Lives in a mid-sized city on the far side of the country from the main city. University student. Shares an apartment with Ben.

**Nick** — Early thirties, Supercub. Visiting Jacob in person for the first time. Civilian clothes, no costume. His bearing energy is still new enough that he doesn't have complete control over it in emotionally charged moments.

**Ben** — Jacob's roommate. Openly gay, though Jacob didn't know this when they moved in together. Attractive, comfortable in himself, into beary guys. Has been quietly carrying feelings for Jacob for over a year without acting on them. Out of the apartment for the duration of the transformation, returns at the end.

---

## Background

Nick and Jacob have been online friends for several years, having met through a shared D&D campaign. They've maintained a genuine friendship beyond the game — voice calls, regular chat, the kind of connection that builds slowly and holds. They have never met in person.

The distance between their cities made it impractical. Neither of them had the means to travel that far, and the logistics never resolved themselves. Nick flying across the country to see Jacob is the first time either of them has closed that gap.

Jacob has seen Nick on webcam — his face, heard his voice, knows him well. What he hasn't seen is the full picture of what Nick looks like now. The transformation that made Nick into Supercub isn't something Jacob knows about. He knows Nick as the friend from the screen.

## Story Structure

### Opening — Nick Arrives

Nick lands outside Jacob's building and switches to civilian mode before knocking. He's been looking forward to this — the simple pleasure of finally meeting someone he already knows well.

Jacob opens the door.

The gap between the Nick he expected and the Nick standing in front of him is immediate and disorienting — not wrong, but vivid. Broad, solid, bearded, completely at home in a body that knows exactly what it is. Jacob registers this without naming it. He lets Nick in, makes coffee, and they fall easily into the rhythm of a friendship that doesn't need warmup.

The ease is real. These are two people who already know each other. The conversation flows without effort.

### Rising Action — The Crack Opens

At some point during the visit, Jacob makes a self-deprecating joke about his body. The kind he has made a thousand times — deflecting before anyone else can comment, keeping the subject light, moving on quickly.

Nick doesn't laugh. He doesn't offer reassurance either. He just waits.

That pause — the specific quality of Nick's attention, his refusal to let the joke land and move on — opens something in Jacob that he didn't intend to open. The image of Nick standing in his doorway earlier, that ease and groundedness, has been sitting in him quietly this whole time.

Jacob says the thing he has never quite said out loud. Not dramatically. Just the quiet, tired truth of what it has been like to live in this body and believe, consistently, that it is a problem to be managed. That he has tried the usual things — diets, a gym phase — and arrived at a settled resignation. That what he wants is to feel comfortable, and that every version of comfortable he can imagine involves being smaller.

Nick listens. He recognises it from the inside — recognises the specific texture of that particular resignation, the way it becomes background noise. He doesn't perform empathy. He just holds it.

### The Trigger — The Hug

Nick pulls Jacob into a hug. Not performing comfort — offering it. Genuine, grounded physical contact between two people who trust each other completely.

And something moves.

Nick's bearing energy, responding to genuine emotional connection and physical contact, crosses the threshold without his permission. He feels it leave him — a warmth, a transfer — and pulls back immediately, alarmed.

Too late.

### The Transformation

Jacob's first response is alarm. Heat spreading under his skin, the unfamiliar pressure of his body beginning to change, the panicked thought that something is wrong with him. He has spent his adult life wishing his body were different. But not like this. Not without understanding what is happening.

He resists it for a moment. His body does not stop.

Then something shifts. The warmth stops feeling like intrusion and starts feeling like recognition. His body is not becoming something foreign. It is becoming something that was structurally true about him all along.

The transformation is full and unhurried:
- His frame widens and deepens, mass redistributing into dense muscle beneath softness that does not disappear
- His belly remains — grounded now, belonging to a body that knows what it is
- Body hair arrives across his chest, stomach, and arms — thick, ginger-auburn, unmistakably beary
- His face changes last: jaw squaring, features settling into something more defined and assured
- The beard comes in dense and full — auburn-ginger with copper highlights — and transforms his face completely

When it's over, Jacob stands looking at his hands. Then he finds the mirror in the hallway.

The horror has passed. What replaces it is something he has no prepared word for. He doesn't look like the version of himself he spent years trying to diet his way toward. He looks like something else entirely.

He looks, for the first time, like himself.

### Nick's Reaction

Nick is shaken. He triggered something irreversible in his friend without intending to. The consent framework matters to him — he's seen what forced transformation looks like from the inside of a Brotherhood cell, and even knowing Jacob is fine, even seeing Jacob in front of the mirror with that expression on his face, Nick has to sit with the fact that something permanent happened and he didn't control it.

Jacob, reading Nick's expression, is the one who reassures him. Which is its own small beat — Jacob, who has spent the whole story being apologetic and self-erasing, telling Nick that it's okay. That it's more than okay. That he doesn't have words for what it's okay.

### The Return — Ben Comes Home

Ben comes home to find Jacob transformed and Nick looking slightly stunned at the kitchen table.

Ben's reaction is not what Jacob expected. He doesn't fall for the new body. He fell for Jacob a long time ago — and what finally comes out, in this strange and charged moment, is that he always had. That the reason he never said anything wasn't Jacob's body. It was his own uncertainty about how Jacob felt.

Jacob has to absorb two things simultaneously: that he was wrong about his body not being worth loving, and that he was wrong about the whole situation being unwinnable from the start.

He takes a moment.

Then he laughs — properly, freely, the laugh of someone who has just discovered they were wrong about something in the best possible way.

Nick, quietly, makes himself scarce.

## Tone

Warm. Intimate. The transformation is joyful but earns its joy — the horror comes first and is real before the recognition arrives. The Ben ending is sweet without being saccharine; it resolves something without tying it too neatly.

This is not a story about a man becoming loveable. It is a story about a man discovering he already was.

## Lore Notes

- This is **Normal Bearing** — Jacob gains a powerful, beary body with presence and physical confidence, but no superpowers.
- The accidental mechanics are lore-consistent: Nick's bearing energy responded to genuine emotional connection and physical contact. The consent is present on Jacob's side — his desire for embodiment, his wanting to feel at home in himself, was real even if his conscious preference was for smallness. Bearing answered the deeper truth.
- Nick's distress afterward is important and should not be skipped. It reflects the consent framework your universe is built on.
- Ben's pre-existing feelings are essential to keeping this body-positive rather than an ugly duckling narrative. Jacob doesn't become loveable. He was already loved.

## What This Story Does for the Universe

- Establishes Nick performing his first bearing — accidentally, privately, outside the superhero context
- Introduces the chubby muscle bear body type, distinct from every other transformation in the series
- Demonstrates that bearing answers deep desire rather than conscious preference
- Expands the universe's geographic and social world beyond the main city
- Shows Nick's emotional complexity around the consent framework post-Brotherhood arc
