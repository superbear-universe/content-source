# When Andrew Got What He Wanted

The lecture ends. Andrew closes his notebook, waits for the first wave of students to clear, and joins the second.

Andrew is twenty, an Italian exchange student, and sharp enough to have figured out how most things work without quite figuring out how to act on any of it. He's been meaning to start going to the campus gym for four months. What stops him isn't laziness — it's that he already knows what he'll see in the mirror when he gets there, and what he won't.

He passes the gym on his way out. Through the glass, guys work the free weights like they own the place. He keeps walking.

He's cutting through the faculty building when he sees Adam.

Adam has been gone for two weeks. Missing seminars, not answering messages. Andrew had meant to follow up.

He almost doesn't recognize him.

The man leaning against the wall outside the admin office is broad, settled, wearing a leather harness over a sleeveless black shirt. His beard is full and deliberate. He looks nothing like someone who went missing. He looks like someone who arrived somewhere.

"Andrew," Adam says.

The name comes out flat. No upward curl at the end.

"Where have you been?" Andrew asks. "I've been carrying the project alone for two weeks."

Adam looks at him evenly. "You managed."

Andrew stares at him. "That's it? You managed?"

Adam says nothing.

"Two weeks," Andrew continues. "No messages, no emails, not even a—" He stops. Looks at him properly. The mass. The beard. The way Adam is standing like the corridor belongs to him. "What happened to you?"

"Training."

"In two weeks."

"Yes."

"That's not possible." Andrew's eyes move over him again, slower this time. "Are you on something? Steroids? What kind of steroids even does this?"

"Nothing like that."

"Then what? Because this—" he gestures at him, "—doesn't just happen."

"My trainer," Adam says. "Come and meet him."

"Who is he?"

"Someone who knew what I needed."

"That's not an answer."

"No," Adam says. "But it's the one that got me here."

He steps closer and puts a hand on Andrew's shoulder — steady, deliberate. The weight of it lands in a way that's difficult to ignore.

"Go on," Adam says quietly. "Feel it."

Andrew hesitates. Then reaches out and grips Adam's arm.

The muscle under his hand is dense and unyielding, completely unlike anything he's felt before. He lets go after a second, like the contact has made the situation more real rather than less.

"That's not steroids," Andrew says, mostly to himself.

"No," Adam says. "It isn't." A beat. "It's not just the body either. Everything's clearer now. Simpler." He looks at Andrew evenly. "You know how much energy you spend just deciding things? What to do, what to say, whether something matters? That's gone for me now."

Andrew looks at him. He doesn't know whether that sounds like freedom or a warning.

"One meeting," Adam says. "That's all."

Andrew doesn't answer straight away. His eyes drift to a glass panel in the corridor wall. His own reflection looks back — tall, lean, unremarkable. The same face he has been looking at for twenty years.

He looks at Adam again.

"Fine," he says quietly. "One meeting."

Adam nods once and walks toward the exit.

Andrew takes one last look at his reflection.

Then he follows.

---

The gym is three streets off the main road. No sign outside, just a matte black plaque beside tinted glass.

Inside, it's quiet. Disciplined. Men move through their sets without performing for anyone. Heads tilt as Adam walks the floor. A few nod once.

At the far end, a man stands near the mirrors with his back half-turned. Broad neck, thick shoulders, a sleeveless black hoodie pushed up at the arms. His head is shaved clean. He turns as they approach, and Andrew gets the full picture — a long black beard, heavy brows, the kind of physical presence that makes the surrounding space feel smaller.

His gaze moves over Andrew once. Steady. Unhurried.

Adam stops three paces back. "Sir."

"You're tall," Bill says.

"I suppose." He hesitates. "I didn't catch your name."

"Bill."

He tilts his head slightly. "How long have you been training?"

"On and off. Four months, maybe."

"What does on look like?"

Andrew pauses. "Machines, mostly. Off-peak hours."

Bill nods, no judgment in it. "And off?"

"Longer than on."

A beat. Bill looks at him the way someone does when they're deciding whether a thing is worth their time — not unkind, just honest.

"What is it you're actually trying to get to?"

Andrew opens his mouth for the safe answer — strength, consistency, improvement — and something in the directness of the question stops him.

"I don't want to look like I'm still figuring it out," he says.

Bill holds his gaze for a moment. Then nods once, like that was the only answer worth giving, and gestures toward a door at the back.

"Come."

---

The back room is smaller. Bare walls, a full-length mirror against one side, a desk. Bill closes the door behind them.

"Mind if I smoke?" he says, already reaching into his bag. "Bad habit. But I own the place and it eases my nerves."

He produces a cigar and lights it without waiting for an answer. The smoke rises in a slow, unhurried line.

Bill circles Andrew once, taking him in the way he did on the gym floor, but closer now.

"How old are you?"

"Twenty."

"Exchange student?"

"Italian. Second year."

Bill nods. "How are you finding it? The city, I mean. Not the program."

Andrew pauses, caught off guard by the question. "Fine. It's a big place. Takes time to find your footing."

"That's honest," Bill says. He leans against the desk. "Most people say they love it."

The smoke drifts between them. Andrew breathes it in without thinking about it. Something in the room feels slower than it did a moment ago, easier, like a conversation he doesn't need to manage.

"Adam said you've been here two years," Bill continues. "You settled in well? Friends, routine, that kind of thing?"

"Mostly," Andrew says. "I know how it works."

Bill looks at him. "But?"

Andrew shrugs. "Knowing how something works and feeling like you belong in it aren't the same thing."

The man is quiet for a moment, like he's sitting with that rather than filing it away.

"No," he says. "They aren't."

He takes a slow draw from the cigar. The ember brightens.

"What I do isn't personal training," he says. "It's not a program, not a routine, not something you maintain. What I do is permanent. One session. You walk out different and you stay that way."

Andrew looks at him. "That's not how bodies work."

"No," Bill agrees. "It isn't."

A beat.

"What changes?" Andrew asks.

"Everything visible," Bill says. "And some things that aren't."

Andrew looks at him. "How does it actually work?"

Bill holds out the cigar. "This is how it starts."

Andrew looks at it. "You want me to start smoking?"

"Just this once."

Andrew looks at it. Then at Bill.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

"How?" Andrew asks.

"Does it matter?" Bill says. "You already know you're going to say yes."

Andrew doesn't answer. The worst part is that he's not wrong.

"I don't smoke," Andrew says finally.

"You don't have to make a habit of it."

A beat. Andrew turns the logic of it over — the back room, the gym outside, Adam on the other side of that door looking like everything Andrew has been waiting to become. None of this is how any of this should work. He knows that.

He takes the cigar anyway.

He draws.

The smoke fills him completely. His eyes burn — brief, sharp, specific. He blinks.

His gaze snaps to the mirror. His eyes — brown this morning, brown his entire life — are turning purple.

He hands the cigar back. The man takes it without comment.

Then it starts.

Heat detonates in his chest without warning — deep, structural, like his body has been given an instruction it cannot refuse. Andrew's breath cuts off. His hands find the edge of the desk and grip it hard.

Then the pain arrives.

His shoulders wrench wide with a force that staggers him, bone and muscle forced outward, pulled apart from the inside. He gasps, mouth open, a sound tearing out of him that he doesn't recognize as his own voice. The veins in his forearms surge to the surface, thick and visible, his knuckles whitening against the desk as his arms begin to thicken — mass arriving with a brutal, indifferent certainty. His shirt splits across the back with a sound like a crack. Andrew flinches, eyes wide, heart slamming so hard he feels it in his throat.

He should stop this.

He knows he should stop this.

Then the heat shifts.

It stops feeling like damage and starts feeling like pressure — enormous, building, almost unbearable. His chest surges forward, pectorals swelling dense and heavy, and underneath the pain something else threads through, warm and electric, moving outward from his core in slow deliberate waves. His blood runs hot, pounding through him with a new weight behind it, his pulse deep and insistent.

Andrew's grip on the desk loosens.

"This feels—" he breathes, voice wrecked. "This feels— *good*—"

The heat floods downward. His core solidifies, weight gathering low and permanent, and he straightens involuntarily, spine pulling upright as if claimed by something. Body hair spreads across his chest and stomach in a wave of prickling heat, dark and dense, and he looks down at himself with something that is no longer fear.

He flexes without deciding to.

His bicep peaks hard and full and the sensation that rolls through him is enormous — pleasure and power arriving together, indistinguishable from each other. He does it again, deliberately this time, watching the mass respond, feeling strength answer back with an immediacy that makes his head swim.

"*Fuck—*" he breathes. Then louder, grinning now: "*Fuck yeah—*"

His thighs press his shorts to their limit. His stance widens, rooted, his body finding its own centre of gravity somewhere lower and more deliberate than it has ever been. The pressure hits his jaw and his face broadens by degrees, heat prickling across his chin and throat as his beard surges in thick and full. He turns toward the mirror.

The man looking back at him is enormous.

He watches his own chest heave, watches the mass settle, watches his face finish changing — and something in him that has been waiting a very long time stops waiting.

He rolls his shoulders. Feels the weight of them. Flexes again, both arms this time, and lets out a long shaking breath that is almost a laugh.

His height was always there. Now something fills it.

The room is smaller. He is not.

He stands there breathing, waiting for the panic to catch up with what just happened.

It doesn't come.

He reaches for the unease he walked in with — about Adam, about the room, about Bill — and finds only quiet. Settled. Gone the way a question dissolves once the answer stops mattering.

He flexes again. Feels the strength answer back. And underneath it, something else stirs — unfamiliar, directionless, like a current looking for a channel. He has no name for it yet.

He doesn't go looking for one.

Behind him, Bill glances over once. "Yes," he says, and turns back to his desk.

Andrew straightens.

He leaves the back room. He walks the floor. Ripping off his tattered t-shirt. Heads tilt as he passes.

He doesn't slow.

---

Outside, evening has settled in. The city moves at the same pace it always has.

His footsteps land differently. More weight. More deliberate. The ground meets him instead of tolerating him.

People adjust their path without looking up.

He catches his reflection in a shop window and stops.

The man in the glass is broad, bearded, settled into himself in a way that takes up space without apology. He looks like what Andrew has been waiting to become for as long as he can remember.

He looks like a man now.

The purple eyes look back, steady and unhurried.

Andrew holds their gaze for a long moment.

Then he walks on into the city, and doesn't look back.
