## Superbear Chapter 13:
# Someone Like That

*Weeks earlier.*

The Den on a Tuesday was a kindness Arun did to himself, and most weeks he forgot why.

The dance floor was raised one step above the bar, edged in a strip of LED that cycled between purple and blue. Half the stools were occupied. Half the bodies on the floor were dancing and the other half were doing the slower thing men did when they wanted to be on a dance floor without committing to it. The music was loud enough to fill the room and quiet enough that you could still hear the bartender call your tab.

Arun was at the bar, third stool from the wall, with a vodka soda he had ordered for the shape of holding it.

He watched.

That was the part he was good at. He had been doing it long enough to know which men in the room would go home with someone and which ones wouldn't, and he could usually call it within ten minutes of walking in. Tonight he had given himself five.

The big one in the henley had come in with friends and would leave with one of them. The shorter guy with the silver rings was already deep in a conversation with the bartender that was going somewhere. The two by the column were a couple, and they were looking.

He cataloged them with the practiced quiet of a man who had stopped expecting to appear in his own list.

He was twenty-eight. He had a job he liked well enough. He had a small apartment with a window that got morning light and a sister who called on Sundays. His shoulders were narrow. His jaw stayed smooth no matter what he did about it. The crowd in here was built for a different shape of man, and he was not that shape, and he knew it.

The purple light passed across the bar in a slow wash and made everyone look better than they were, which was the cruelest thing the lighting did. It made him feel like he was watching a film he hadn't been cast in.

He took a sip of the drink he wasn't really drinking.

He gave himself one more song.

---

The door opened and the air in the room rearranged itself.

Arun felt it before he saw it. A shift in the angle of a few heads at the bar. A pause in two conversations near the entrance. The bartender, who never looked up for anyone, looked up.

Then Arun saw him.

The man who had walked in was the biggest person in the room, and he was not trying to be. Tall, broad through the shoulders and chest in a way that made the doorway look small behind him. Strawberry-blonde hair, longish and wavy. A full auburn beard, trimmed close. Fair skin pinked at the cheekbones from the cold outside. He was wearing a black sleeveless tank that fit him better than any garment had any business fitting anyone. The light caught the surface of it and slid off — high-gloss, glassy, almost wet-looking. Latex, Arun realized. Dark grey cargo trousers, a black belt, heavy lace-up boots.

He wore it like other men wore a t-shirt. No performance in it. No checking himself.

He moved into the room without scanning it. He didn't need to scan it. He took two steps in and the room had already noticed him and rearranged what it was doing to accommodate the noticing.

Arun looked away first, the way he had trained himself to look away first.

He stared into his drink and felt the ridiculous heat in his face and told himself, very precisely, *don't*. Don't watch. Don't catalog. Don't put him on the list of men you're going to think about later and feel worse for having thought about. You know how this ends. It ends with you here at the bar and him with someone else, and the only difference between this Tuesday and the last Tuesday is the specific shape of the ache.

He gave himself a few seconds. Then he looked up.

The man was looking at him.

Arun's first thought was that he had misread the angle. He glanced at the woman two stools down to confirm. She was on her phone, oblivious. He glanced at the couple by the column. They were facing the other direction. He looked back, slower this time, sure he was about to catch the moment the man's eyes shifted to whoever was actually being looked at.

The man was looking at him. Direct and unhurried.

Arun's stomach did something low and unfamiliar.

He had been looked at before, in a manner of speaking. He had been looked past more often. On three or four occasions in his entire adult life he had been looked at the way he was being looked at right now, which was to say, as if he were the answer to a question someone had been carrying around. Each of those previous occasions had turned out to be a mistake. A man finding his actual target somewhere over Arun's shoulder. A moment of accidental eye contact that the other man broke as soon as he realized.

This wasn't breaking. The man held his eyes, and after a moment that lasted longer than it should have, he tilted his chin in a small, unmistakable gesture: *come here.*

Arun didn't move.

The man waited a beat. Then he smiled — small, warm, patient — and started toward him instead.

The walk took maybe six seconds. Arun used those seconds to invent and discard several plausible explanations for what was happening, including the possibility that he had fallen asleep at the bar and this was the part of the dream where his brain was being kind to him for the first time in months.

The man arrived at the empty stool beside him and sat down. Sitting, he was still bigger than most people standing.

"Hi," he said.

His voice was lower than Arun had braced for, and warmer.

"Hi," Arun said.

The man looked at him for a second longer, taking him in without rushing it, and Arun felt his own face being read in a way that should have made him want to look away and somehow didn't.

"What are you drinking?"

"Vodka soda."

"You've been holding it for an hour."

Arun felt the heat hit his face again. "You've been here ten minutes."

"I was watching the door before I came in."

There was no good answer to that, and Arun had the sudden, absurd sense that no answer was required of him. The man had said it as a small gift, as if to put them on equal footing. *I saw you first. I came in for you.* He couldn't decide if it was a line. He couldn't decide if it mattered.

"What's your name?" the man asked.

"Arun."

"Arun." He said it once, carefully, as if checking the shape of it. "I'm Nick."

He held out his hand.

Arun shook it.

The hand was warm and broad and dry, and the pressure of it was exactly correct, and Arun let it go a fraction of a second after he should have. Nick noticed and didn't comment on it. The corner of his mouth moved.

"You here with anyone?" Nick asked.

"No."

"Good."

The word landed in the middle of Arun's chest and stayed there.

Nick didn't fill the silence after it. He let it sit. He looked at Arun while it sat, and the look did the thing the look had been doing since the door — direct, unhurried, hungry in a way that wasn't ugly. It was the hunger of a man who had decided. Arun, who had spent eight years being looked past, did not have the equipment to defend himself against being looked at like that. He hadn't built it. There had been no occasion.

Nick's hand came to rest on Arun's forearm. Not gripping. Just there. The weight of it, the heat through the thin sleeve, the size of his palm. Arun's pulse jumped under the touch and Nick could feel it. Arun knew he could feel it. Nick's thumb moved once, slow, along the inside of Arun's wrist, and Arun's breath caught audibly enough that the bartender, two feet away, pretended not to hear.

"You want to get out of here?" Nick said.

It wasn't a real question. It was a check. A formality. He already knew.

"Yes," Arun said.

Nick stood. His hand slid from Arun's wrist to the small of his back as Arun got off the stool, and the contact was steady and proprietary, and Arun didn't pull away from any of it. He left the vodka soda on the bar and didn't look back at it.

Nick guided him toward the rear of the club, past the bathrooms, past a service door propped open with a wooden wedge. The bouncer at the back glanced up and went back to his phone. The hand at Arun's back was the only thing in the building Arun was aware of.

The door swung shut behind them and the music dropped to a muffled bass through the wall, and they were in the alley.

It was cold. The kind of cold that found the gap between his collar and his neck immediately. The alley was narrow and dim and smelled like the dumpster against the far wall. A single security light buzzed above the service door in a pale yellow that made everything look worse than it was. The brick of the back wall was wet from earlier rain.

Arun registered all of it in one quick sweep and then stopped registering any of it.

Nick was looking at him again.

In the yellow light, with the music a low pulse through the wall behind him, Nick was somehow more, not less. The pale eyes had a tint to them that the club lighting had washed out — a violet undertone Arun would have called a trick of the bulb if he had been thinking about it, which he wasn't. The latex of the tank caught what little light there was and held it.

"Come here," Nick said.

Arun came here.

Nick's hand found the back of his neck and brought his face up, and Nick bent his head and kissed him.

The kiss was warm and unhurried and entirely sure of itself. Arun's back found the brick wall behind him without his having moved toward it, which meant Nick had moved him there, gently, while kissing him. The full weight and size of Nick was against him now. The latex of the tank was warm where it pressed against the front of Arun's shirt — warmer than skin should have been, as if Nick had been somewhere hot recently, which he hadn't.

Arun kissed him back. He had not kissed anyone in fourteen months. The part of him that kept track of that fact lost interest in keeping track of it.

---

He didn't notice the moment Nick's hand moved to his chest. He noticed that it was there.

Bare palm, flat, broad, pressed over his sternum through the thin fabric of his shirt. The heat of it was the same heat as the rest of him, which by now was everywhere. His mouth, his jaw, his neck, the long line where their bodies met. Arun made a small sound into the kiss without meaning to. Nick swallowed it.

Then the heat under the palm changed.

It was a small change at first. A coolness where the warmth had been. A different texture against his shirt. The give of his shirt against a hand that was no longer quite a hand.

Arun's mouth slowed against Nick's. He pulled back half an inch, eyes still closed, trying to place the wrongness.

He opened his eyes and looked down.

Nick's hand on his chest was sheathed in glossy black latex.

It hadn't been there a second ago. Arun had shaken that hand at the bar. It had been a hand. It was a hand encased in the same impossible glassy material as the tank top, and it was pressed flat against the front of Arun's shirt, and as Arun watched — as the part of him that could still think tried to assemble what he was looking at — the latex began to move.

Not the hand. The latex.

It crossed from the glove onto the fabric of his shirt where the palm met his chest, and from the fabric it slid onto his skin underneath, and from his skin it spread. A thin glossy film racing outward from the contact point. Across his chest. Over his collarbone. The shirt going with it or under it or through it. Arun couldn't tell which and it didn't matter, because the rubber was on him, and inside the place where the shirt had been, and claiming the surface of his body in a steady wave that started at Nick's gloved hand and didn't stop.

His brain offered him several useless thoughts in quick succession. He discarded them.

He looked up at Nick.

Nick's eyes were on his face, and Nick's pupils were doing something Arun did not have a word for, and Nick's breath against Arun's cheek was uneven in a way it had not been a moment earlier. A low sound came up out of Nick's throat. Not a kiss-sound. Deeper. From further down. The hand on Arun's chest pressed harder. The fingers spread, claiming more surface, and the rubber raced faster from the new contact.

Nick's whole body shuddered against him. Once, then again.

The shudder came through the latex of the tank where their chests met, and through Nick's hand on his chest, and Arun, who had kissed exactly five men in his life and slept with three of them, had never in his life been this close to a man who was getting *this much* out of touching him.

And the part of Arun that was still Arun — the part that watched, that cataloged, that had spent his whole adult life studying men's faces for signs of interest — recognized the wrongness of the shape of it.

*He's not getting it from me.*

*He's getting it from this.*

The hand. The shudder. The way Nick's breath had changed at the exact instant the latex had appeared on his palm. None of it was about Arun. Arun was the place where the thing was happening, and the thing happening was what Nick wanted.

The look he had taken for wanting was a different kind of hunger.

That recognition arrived clean and complete, and was the last clean, complete thought Arun had.

The rubber reached his throat.

Something deep in his body let go of what it had been holding for a long time, and the chronic low ache he had carried — the everyday ache of being himself in a room — went quiet. Not muted. Quiet. A volume dial turning down on a noise he had stopped noticing was there until it was gone.

His chest filled out under the rubber. He felt it happen. His ribcage broadened. The narrow shoulders he had spent his whole life apologizing for pushed outward against the spreading film and the film accommodated them. Mass arrived where mass had never been. His arms thickened inside his sleeves and then there were no sleeves, only the rubber, sealing tight along the new shape of him as he became it. His jaw prickled. He brought a hand up — slowly, the gesture unfamiliar already because the arm doing it was heavier than the arm he had reached with a minute ago — and felt the beard coming in under his fingers. The beard he had never been able to grow.

It should have hurt. It didn't. The body saying yes to what was happening was louder than any other signal it had. Pleasure rolled through him in long structural waves that started in the bones, and the pleasure was indistinguishable from the changing, and the changing was indistinguishable from the simplification — fewer thoughts, smaller, the noise of being himself easing into a clean steady hum.

And Nick, the whole time, pressed against him. Shuddering. Feeding.

Arun's last moment of recognizable interiority was not about himself. It was the awareness, dim and accepting, that the man holding him to the wall was being rewarded for what was being done to him, and that the reward and the doing were the same act.

Then the rubber closed over his eyes, and behind it the violet came on, and Arun was gone.

---

What stood against the brick wall was bigger than what had walked out of the club.

The full-body suit was seamless and glossy and total, neck to boots, every contour of a thickly built man rendered in high-shine black. The chest was deep. The shoulders were wide enough now to fill the alley properly. The beard had come in dark and full along a jaw that was no longer narrow. The eyes were open and steady, the same cold violet as Nick's, set in a face that wore the calm settled expression of a man who had been relieved of a burden he hadn't known the weight of until it was gone.

He stood where Arun had stood. He did not move. He was waiting to be told what came next.

⁂

The first thing Nick was aware of was the heat under his skin going slow and bright.

It rolled up out of his chest and along his arms and down his spine in long warm pulses, deep and structural, and he closed his eyes and let it. The suit was praising him. It did this every time and every time it was new. The pleasure was not in any one place. It was the whole of him, lit from inside, the body of a man who had done what he was made for and was being told so.

He breathed out, slow, through his nose.

The hunger was quieter now. He had eaten.

He opened his eyes.

The new one stood against the brick wall, finished. Big. Settled. The violet in his eyes was clean and steady, and the rubber on him caught what little light the alley had and held it the same as Nick's own.

*Mine. I made that.*

The thought was simple and total and felt good in his mouth, even though he hadn't said it.

He looked down at his own hand.

The glove was retracting. He watched it happen — the glossy black surface receding up his fingers in a slow smooth pull, sliding back over his palm, easing up his wrist, sinking through the skin where it lived between feedings. He flexed the hand once when it was done. The skin was bare. Unmarked. A hand.

He had done this before. He would do it again before the night was over.

He turned his head toward the new one.

"Wait," he said.

The new one didn't answer. He didn't need to. He stood where he had been told to stand.

Nick turned toward the service door. The bass of the music was coming through the wall in a steady low pulse he could feel in his boots. Past the door was the warm yellow light of the back corridor, and past that, the room, and in the room, the rest of them.

He could feel the hunger already starting to come back, on a long slow timer, and he liked the feeling of it coming.

He pulled the door open.

The music got loud again.

He walked back inside.
