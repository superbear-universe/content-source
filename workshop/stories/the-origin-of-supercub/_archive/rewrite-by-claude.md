## Superbear Chapter 9:
# The Origin of Supercub

The TV had been on when Nick woke up. He didn't remember Mike turning it on. He didn't remember much of anything from the last few hours except the way the safe house bed had felt too soft, and how Mike's hand had stayed on his shoulder until he'd finally stopped shaking.

Now the afternoon light cut through the apartment windows at wrong angles, too bright, making Nick squint. The city hummed outside like nothing had happened. Like the world had already moved on.

On screen: shaky phone footage. A warehouse wall exploding outward in a cloud of concrete dust. The blue-and-red blur of Superbear tearing through it like paper.

Then the footage stabilized. Zoomed.

Superbear, walking out of the rubble. Carrying someone.

Nick's stomach dropped.

The body in Mike's arms hung limp, head lolled back. The camera couldn't get a good angle on the face. Good. Nick didn't want to see his own face like that, didn't want to know what he'd looked like when—

*Unidentified individual*, the chyron read.

The anchor's voice continued, flat and professional. "—significant structural damage to the waterfront facility. Authorities have declined to comment on whether criminal charges—"

The TV went black.

Nick looked up. Mike stood by the wall, hand still on the power button, shoulders rigid.

Neither of them spoke.

The silence wasn't comfortable. It sat between them like something physical, something that took up space and had weight. Nick could feel his heartbeat in his throat. Could feel the bruises on his wrists where the restraints had been, even though Mike had healed those. Could feel—

"You hungry?" Mike asked. His voice came out wrong. Too casual. "I could make something. Or order. Whatever you want."

Nick stared at the blank TV screen.

"They knew," he said.

Mike went still. "Nick—"

"They knew exactly what they were doing." The words felt distant, like someone else was saying them. "I keep thinking— I keep trying to figure out why they even bothered keeping me awake. You know? They could've just knocked me out. Would've been easier."

"Don't," Mike said quietly.

"But they didn't." Nick's hands were shaking. He pressed them against his thighs. "They wanted me to know. Wanted me scared. Wanted me to—" His breath caught. "Why would they do that unless—"

"Stop."

"Unless they *wanted* you to know I was scared." The thought finished itself. Completed. "They needed you angry. They needed you stupid."

Mike's jaw clenched. "That's not what—"

"I'm bait." Nick laughed, and it sounded shrill even to his own ears. "I'm just bait. I'm the thing they use to make you do what they want."

"No," Mike said firmly.

"What happens next time?" Nick was on his feet now, didn't remember standing. "What happens when it's not some warehouse, when they're smarter about it, when they—" His voice cracked. "I can't do this."

"You won't have to."

"How?" Nick turned on him. "How, Mike? You going to watch me every second? Put me in a bunker somewhere? I can't— I can't live like that. I can't be the reason you—"

He stopped. Sat down hard on the couch. Put his head in his hands.

Mike crossed the room slowly, like approaching something breakable. He sat on the coffee table, facing Nick, close enough to touch but not touching.

"I can protect you," he said.

Nick looked up. "For how long?"

The question hung there.

Mike's expression cracked. Just for a second, but Nick saw it. The fear underneath. The helplessness.

"I don't know," Mike admitted. "But I'll figure it out. I'll—"

"Unless I wasn't—" Nick stopped. Shook his head. "No. That's insane."

"What?"

"Nothing."

Mike's eyes narrowed. "Nick."

"It's not— it doesn't matter."

"Tell me anyway."

Nick stood up, paced to the window. Looked out at the city. At all those people down there, living normal lives, going about their days, not being bait for supervillains.

"Unless I wasn't human," he said to the glass.

The silence behind him changed quality.

"No," Mike said.

Nick turned. "You could—"

"Absolutely not." Mike was on his feet, hands raised like he could physically stop the conversation. "That's not— we're not talking about this."

"Why not?"

"Because it's not something you just *do*, Nick. Because it's permanent. Because I won't—" Mike's voice broke. "I won't do that to you."

"What if I'm asking you to?"

"You don't know what you're asking."

"Then tell me." Nick moved closer. "Explain it to me. What happens? What does it mean?"

Mike backed up a step. Actually backed away from him. "Nick, please—"

"I'm not made of glass, Bear. Tell me."

Something in Mike's face crumpled. He dragged both hands through his hair, gripped it, let go. Turned away. Turned back.

"It changes everything," he said finally. "Your body. Your cells. It's not— you don't get to go back. You don't get to change your mind. And it's not just strength or flight or any of that, it's being *visible*. It's being a target. It's people looking at you and seeing a symbol or a threat or a— a thing they can use." His voice was rising. "It's waking up and knowing you'll never be normal again. Never be safe. Never be able to just *exist* without someone having an opinion about it."

"Sounds like you're describing my life already," Nick said quietly.

Mike flinched like he'd been hit.

"The being a target part," Nick clarified. "I'm already that. The only difference is right now I can't do anything about it."

"That's not— this isn't the solution—"

"What is?"

Mike had no answer.

Nick took a breath. Let it out slow. "Tell me the rest. The physical part. What actually happens?"

"Nick—"

"Please."

So Mike told him.

It took a long time. He kept stopping, looking at Nick like he was waiting for him to realize how insane this was and back out. But Nick just listened. Asked questions. Made Mike explain about the transformation process, the permanence, the way the body changed on a cellular level and never changed back.

When Mike finally ran out of words, they both stood there in the middle of the apartment, not touching, barely breathing.

"I need to think," Nick said.

Mike nodded too quickly. "Yeah. Yes. You should. Take however long you—"

"I'm going to do it."

"—need to..." Mike stopped. Stared. "What?"

"I'm going to do it," Nick repeated. "I'm choosing this."

"You can't— you just said you needed to think—"

"I did think." Nick moved closer. "I've been thinking about it since I woke up in that warehouse. Since I realized what I was. What I'd always be to anyone who wants to hurt you." He held Mike's gaze. "I'm not choosing power, Mike. I'm choosing not to be helpless."

Mike's eyes were wide, almost panicked. "You don't have to do this. We can find another way. We can—"

"There is no other way." Nick said it gently. "And I think you know that."

"I can't—" Mike's voice broke completely. "What if something goes wrong? What if I hurt you? What if you regret it and I'm the reason you—"

Nick grabbed his face. Both hands, firm, making Mike look at him.

"I am choosing this," he said clearly. "Not because I'm scared. Not because I don't have options. Because I want to stand next to you. Because I want to be able to fight back. Because I'm tired of being the thing that makes you vulnerable." He leaned his forehead against Mike's. "I'm choosing *you*. Do you understand?"

Mike's breath shuddered out. "Yeah," he whispered. "Yeah, I understand."

"So are you going to help me or not?"

For a long moment, Mike just stood there, eyes closed, forehead pressed to Nick's.

Then he straightened. Took a breath. Let it out.

"Okay," he said. "Okay."

He didn't move immediately. Neither did. They stood there in the afternoon light, Nick's hands still on Mike's face, Mike's hands hovering near Nick's sides like he didn't know where to put them.

"Now?" Nick asked.

Mike nodded. "Now. Before I lose my nerve."

Nick let his hands drop. Mike caught them, held them for a second too long, then let go.

"It's going to feel strange," Mike said. "Overwhelming, probably. If you need me to stop—"

"I won't."

"If you need me to stop," Mike repeated firmly, "I will. Promise me you'll tell me."

"I promise."

Mike took another breath. Stepped closer. "Okay. I'm going to— it works best through sustained contact. So I'm going to hold you. Is that okay?"

Nick almost laughed. "Mike. Yes."

Mike pulled him in.

It wasn't gentle. Mike's arms wrapped around him tight, almost crushing, like he was trying to hold every part of Nick at once. Nick's face pressed into Mike's shoulder, Mike's chin hooked over Nick's head, their bodies flush from chest to thigh.

For a moment, nothing happened. Just the two of them, breathing in sync.

Then the air changed.

Nick felt it first as a shift in temperature— not hot or cold, just *different*. Then light bloomed at the edges of his vision, rainbow-bright even through his closed eyes. He could feel it against his skin where Mike touched him, a gentle pressure that slowly, slowly started to increase.

"Okay?" Mike's voice rumbled through his chest.

"Yeah," Nick managed. "I'm okay."

The pressure built.

It didn't hurt. It was just *there*, insistent, spreading through him like warmth through water. Nick's breath deepened without him meaning to, his lungs expanding to take in more air than they ever had before.

His heart kicked hard.

Once. Twice. A rhythm that felt too big for his ribcage.

Then something in his chest *moved*.

Not pain. Not quite. Just— wrongness. Rightness. Both at once. Like his body was remembering how to be something it had forgotten.

Mike's arms tightened fractionally. "Stay with me."

Nick tried to answer but his throat didn't work right. The warmth was spreading faster now, radiating out from his core to his limbs. His skin felt too small. His bones felt too light.

His shoulders twitched.

"I've got you," Mike murmured. "I've got you, I'm right here."

The warmth surged.

Nick gasped. His shoulders pushed outward, broadening, and Mike's arms shifted to accommodate the change. Mass arrived in waves— not sudden, not violent, just *inevitable*. His back stretched wider. His chest swelled forward against Mike's.

His shirt pulled tight.

"Mike—" His voice came out deeper. Rougher.

"I know. It's okay. You're okay."

The fabric ripped.

Nick felt it tear across his shoulders, his arms, heard the seams give way as his biceps thickened and his forearms corded with muscle that hadn't been there seconds ago. The sensation was bizarre— like watching yourself in a mirror while someone else controlled your body.

Mike's grip loosened.

Nick made a sound of protest, but Mike was already adjusting, hands sliding down to Nick's waist as he stepped back half a pace.

"I need to see," Mike said roughly. "Need to make sure you're—"

He stopped.

Nick stood there, breathing hard, shirt hanging in tatters. He looked down at himself— at the chest that had filled out heavy and solid, at the arms that looked like they belonged to someone else, at the stomach pushing forward with muscle and mass that felt *earned* somehow, despite arriving in seconds.

"Holy shit," he breathed.

Mike made a strangled noise.

Nick's head snapped up. "What? What's wrong?"

"Nothing," Mike said faintly. "Nothing's wrong. You're just—" He gestured helplessly. "You're gorgeous."

Heat flooded Nick's face. Or maybe that was just the transformation. Hard to tell. The warmth was everywhere now, rolling through him in waves that made his skin prickle and his hair stand on end.

Actually stand on end.

Nick touched his jaw and felt thick beard under his palm. Felt it *growing*, pushing through skin that tingled with sensitivity.

"Oh my god," he laughed, breathless and shocked. "Oh my god, is this—"

The warmth surged again, stronger this time.

Rainbow light blazed around them, bright enough that Nick had to close his eyes. The pressure that had been building in his chest suddenly expanded outward, filling every cell, every atom. His body felt too full and too empty at the same time, like a vessel being filled with something that had no regard for the laws of physics.

His feet left the floor.

"Nick!" Mike's voice, sharp with alarm.

Nick's eyes flew open. He was floating. Actually floating, hovering a foot off the ground while rainbow light poured off him in waves.

"I don't— I can't—" His breath came in short gasps. The pressure was still building. "Mike, I can't control—"

"It's okay!" Mike rose to meet him, hands finding Nick's arms. "You don't have to control it. Just let it happen. Let it finish."

"What if—"

"I'm right here," Mike said firmly. "I won't let you fall."

The light intensified until Nick couldn't see anything but white-rainbow-gold, until the humming in his bones became a roar, until he couldn't tell where his body ended and the light began.

Then everything *locked*.

The sensation was instant and absolute— like a key turning in a mechanism that spanned his entire body. Every cell clicking into its final configuration. Every atom settling into permanent alignment.

The light didn't fade so much as it *withdrew*, pulling back into Nick's skin like it had never been outside him at all. The roar in his ears dropped to a hum, then to silence.

Nick drifted downward.

His feet touched the floor with a solidity that felt new. Final. The apartment creaked under his weight in a way it never had before.

He stood there, eyes closed, breathing carefully.

"Nick?" Mike's voice was small. Scared.

Nick opened his eyes.

The apartment looked the same. Mike looked the same. But Nick— Nick felt different in every possible way. Heavier. Stronger. More *real*, somehow, like he'd been a rough draft before and now he was the finished version.

He looked down.

His ruined clothes were gone. In their place: a suit. Blue and yellow, fitting perfectly across his broader chest and shoulders. A red cape pooling on the floor behind him. A red belt at his waist.

He touched the fabric. It felt real. Felt right.

"Say something," Mike whispered. "Please."

Nick looked up at him. At the fear and hope and wonder all fighting for space on Mike's face. At the way Mike was holding himself so carefully, like he was terrified of what Nick might say.

Nick's chest felt too full. His eyes stung.

"I'm okay," he said. His voice came out deeper, steadier. "I'm— Mike, I'm okay."

Mike let out a sob.

Then he was moving, closing the distance between them, hands reaching for Nick's face like he'd been holding himself back and couldn't anymore. His palms cupped Nick's jaw, fingers sliding into the thick beard, and Mike just *looked* at him.

"You're beautiful," Mike breathed. "You're so—" His voice cracked. "I'm so proud of you."

Nick tried to laugh but it came out wet. "Yeah?"

"Yeah." Mike pulled him close, slower this time, giving Nick room to pull back if he wanted to.

Nick didn't pull back.

He surged forward and kissed Mike hard, one hand fisting in Mike's shirt and the other wrapping around his back. The kiss was messy and desperate and perfect. Mike kissed him back like he was trying to memorize the taste of him, like he couldn't believe Nick was real and solid and *here*.

They broke apart gasping.

"Hi," Nick said stupidly.

Mike laughed, teary and wrecked. "Hi."

They stood there, foreheads pressed together, breathing each other's air. Nick could feel the new weight of his body, the strength in his arms, the way the cape moved behind him.

He could also feel Mike trembling.

"You're shaking," Nick murmured.

"I was so scared," Mike admitted. "The whole time. I was terrified I'd hurt you. That you'd hate it. That you'd—"

"Hey." Nick pulled back enough to meet his eyes. "I'm right here. I'm not going anywhere."

Mike's breath shuddered out. "I know. I know. It's just—" He gestured at Nick, at all of him. "You're— this is—"

"Permanent," Nick finished quietly.

"Yeah."

"Good." Nick meant it. He could feel it in his bones, in the solid certainty of his new body. "I don't want to go back."

Mike searched his face. Looking for doubt, probably. For regret.

He wouldn't find any.

"Okay," Mike said finally. "Okay."

They stood there a moment longer. Then Nick shifted his weight experimentally— and kept shifting, kept rising, his feet leaving the floor as naturally as breathing.

Mike rose with him. Not on purpose, Nick thought. Just because their bodies remembered each other, remembered how to move together.

They floated in the middle of the apartment, suspended in afternoon light.

"This is insane," Nick said.

"Yeah," Mike agreed.

"I can fly."

"Yeah."

"I'm a—" Nick stopped. Looked down at himself again. At the suit. The cape. The body that felt powerful and new and *his*. "I don't know what I am."

Mike's hands were still on his face. "You're you," he said firmly. "That's all you need to be."

Nick looked at him. At Mike's face, open and honest and full of love that Nick could finally, *finally* match. Could stand beside. Could protect.

"Supercub," he said suddenly.

Mike blinked. "What?"

"That's—" Nick laughed, shaking his head. "I'm Supercub. Right? That's how this works?"

Mike stared at him. Then something bright and helpless broke across his face. "Yeah," he said, voice rough with emotion. "Yeah, that's how it works."

"Superbear and Supercub," Nick tested. "That's ridiculous."

"Completely ridiculous," Mike agreed.

"I love it."

"Me too."

They drifted higher, slowly, until they bumped gently against the ceiling. Nick looked down at the apartment below them— at the couch where he'd sat this morning, at the TV that had shown him his own broken body, at the coffee table and the kitchen and the windows full of afternoon light.

It all looked very small from up here.

Very far away.

"What now?" Nick asked.

Mike took his hand. Laced their fingers together. "Now we figure it out," he said. "Together."

Nick squeezed his hand back.

"Together," he agreed.

They floated there a while longer, not quite ready to come down.

Not quite ready for whatever came next.

But ready enough.

And this time, neither of them would disappear.
