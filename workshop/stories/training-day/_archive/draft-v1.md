### **Chapter 10 — Training day

Steam filled the bathroom in soft white clouds, curling against tile and glass, blurring the edges of the small space until it felt suspended from the rest of the apartment. Water poured steadily from the showerhead, warm and heavy, sliding over Nick’s shoulders, tracing down his back, gathering in the deeper planes of a body that still felt new every time he moved.

He stood beneath it with his eyes closed at first, letting sensation come before thought.

Heat soaked into muscle. Into thickness. Into weight.

His hands moved slowly, almost reverently, spreading soap across skin that responded with warmth and a low, pleasant tingling. His chest felt broad beneath his palms, firm and solid, hair darkened by water and clinging in soft curls against muscle. He traced the slope of his pecs, the deep line where they met his shoulders, the powerful fullness of his arms. Everything felt substantial in a way that invited touch. Not for show. Just because it felt good to exist inside it.

The water caught in his beard, heavier than he expected, dripping from the thick hairs in warm streams. He laughed softly under his breath, shaking his head once and watching droplets scatter against the tile.

A memory drifted up through the warmth.

Waking earlier in the bed.

The mattress pressing firmly into his thighs. The way his body had felt bigger even in sleep, heavier, fuller, powerful without tension. The way simply rolling onto his side had taken more intention, more presence.

He smiled to himself beneath the water.

He finished rinsing slowly, savoring the warmth, then reached carefully for the knob. The first time he’d turned it on earlier that morning, he’d nearly twisted it clean off the wall. Now he adjusted his grip deliberately, easing the pressure until the water slowed, then stopped.

Steam continued to drift upward as he stepped out.

The mirror across from the shower was fogged completely, his reflection a blurred, enlarged silhouette behind the glass.

He lifted one hand and drew a slow line through the fogged mirror.

The reflection sharpened in a vertical streak.

Broad shoulders emerged first.  
Then his chest.  
Then his face.

Nick inhaled softly.

He reached for the towel and wrapped it low around his waist out of instinct, the familiar gesture grounding the unfamiliar body. The fabric barely met across his hips now, held easily in place by the width of him rather than tension.

Then, suddenly, another realization surfaced.

He blinked.

Clear.

Perfectly clear.

Nick froze.

“My glasses…” he murmured.

They were still on his nightstand. He wasn't wearing them.

And yet the world looked crisp. He saw his face in full detail. Droplets on the mirror caught light like tiny prisms.

A quiet laugh escaped him.

“So that’s new too.”

The transformation had fixed his eyesight.

Of course it had.

Everything else had changed. Why not that?

Then he looked fully.

Same eyes.

Same shape to his smile.

Different gravity behind it.

His beard framed his face thick and full, darker than before, catching light in soft highlights where water still clung. He lifted one hand and ran his fingers into it, disappearing into the warmth and density of it with a quiet, pleased breath.

“So this is really happening,” he whispered.

His fingers traced along his jaw, down the strong line of his throat, feeling the pulse there, steady and deep.

Then his arms.

He lifted one experimentally and flexed.

Muscle surged without effort, thickening and tightening beneath skin that stretched smoothly over power. The movement felt natural. Easy. Like his body understood strength now instead of negotiating with it.

“Whoa,” he breathed.

He turned slightly, watching how his shoulders rolled with weight, how his torso shifted with mass, how space adjusted around him.

Water continued to drip from his hair and beard, tapping softly against tile.

A small, private smile curved his mouth.

From the kitchen, faint sounds drifted in — a cupboard opening, a mug set down, the soft hum of a coffee machine warming.

Life continuing.

Nick dried off, the towel already damp from the sheer amount of him, then padded back into the bedroom. On the bed lay clothes Mike had set out carefully.

An oversized t-shirt that now looked like it belonged to someone his size.  
Borrowed sweatpants sitting loose and comfortable.  
Clean white socks.

He pulled them on slowly.

The shirt slid over his chest snug instead of baggy, hugging muscle and belly in a way that felt oddly comforting. The pants settled low on his hips, warm and soft. The fabric carried a faint trace of Mike’s scent — coffee, soap, something warm and familiar.

Nick took a breath and smiled again.

Then he stepped back into the world.

---

Morning light filled the kitchen in warm gold stripes across the counter. Mike stood near the stove, shoulders relaxed, one hand on a pan, the other holding a spatula.

Sausages sizzled. Eggs cooked beside them. A stack of pancakes waited on a plate already threatening to topple.

Mike turned when he heard Nick’s footsteps.

And paused.

Not in shock.

In recognition.

In relief.

In something that looked like pride.

“Morning, Cub,” Mike said softly.

Nick shifted his weight, suddenly aware of how big he felt in borrowed clothes. “Morning.”

Mike smiled wider. “You look… settled.”

Nick considered that.

“Yeah,” he said. “I think I feel it too.”

Mike gestured toward the table. “Fuel up. Big beary body needs big beary breakfast.”

Nick groaned immediately. “You’re already doing this?”

Mike grinned unapologetically. “It’s pawsitive reinforcement.”

“That was awful.”

“And yet,” Mike said, sliding a plate toward him, “effective.”

Nick laughed and sat.

The chair held easily.

He lifted the mug Mike had poured for him and blinked.

It felt almost weightless.

“Huh,” he murmured.

Strength revealed itself in tiny ways.

Balance shifted differently when he leaned back. Sounds seemed sharper — the sizzle of oil, the faint hum of the fridge, distant traffic.

Then, as he went to set the mug back down, his grip tightened a fraction too much.

The ceramic shattered in his hand.

Coffee splashed across the table and his lap.

Nick froze.

“Oh—oh no—”

He waited for pain.

None came.

Just warmth.

And surprise.

“I didn’t even feel it,” he said softly.

Mike was already moving, grabbing a towel, gently wiping the table.

“First morning super strength,” he said kindly. “I broke three chairs and a sink faucet my first day.”

Nick looked up at him. “Seriously?”

“Seriously.”

Nick exhaled a shaky laugh. “Okay. That makes me feel better.”

They cleaned up together, easy and domestic.

Nick glanced at Mike, then said quietly, clearly, deliberately:

“Can you show me how you learned to use this?”

Mike didn’t hesitate.

“Yeah. Of course.”

Relief spread through Nick’s chest.

“Finish eating,” Mike added. “Then we’ll head to the rooftop.”

Nick frowned slightly. “The rooftop?”

“You’ll see.”

---

The elevator ride felt longer than usual, Nick hyper-aware of the hum of machinery beneath his feet. When the doors opened, sunlight spilled across concrete and greenery.

The rooftop terrace stretched wide, dotted with plants, benches, and the low mechanical bulk of HVAC units.

The city spread beyond it in every direction.

The air hit Nick all at once.

Smells layered instantly — fresh leaves, hot concrete, distant traffic, coffee from a nearby café.

Sounds stacked on top of each other — birds, voices, engines, wind.

Nick staggered half a step.

“Whoa—”

Mike was at his side immediately, steadying him with a warm hand at his back.

“Breathe,” Mike said softly. “Focus on one thing. Pick a sound.”

Nick closed his eyes.

Found the wind.

Let everything else blur.

The world softened.

“Okay,” he breathed. “That helped.”

“Your senses are boosted,” Mike said. “They’ll feel intense at first. Your brain learns how to filter.”

Nick nodded slowly.

Then Mike smiled.

“Next lesson. The suit.”

They stood facing each other in the open sun.

“You already figured part of it out in your sleep,” Mike said. “Your subconscious told the bear energy you wanted to be in your own skin again.”

Nick blinked. “So it listens to what I feel?”

“Pretty much.”

Mike explained the warmth, the gathering in the chest, the release.

Nick tried.

At first nothing happened.

Then he felt it — a deep glow pooling beneath his sternum, spreading outward, building.

“Okay… okay, I feel it…”

“Let it grow. Then release.”

Nick focused.

The rainbow light burst outward in rolling waves, washing over him in warm brilliance.

In a flash, the super suit wrapped perfectly around his body.

Nick gasped.

“Oh my god.”

The fabric felt flexible, strong, comfortable — like it moved with him instead of against him.

“I could nap in this,” Nick said in awe.

Mike laughed. “Same. Lazy-day pajamas of destiny.”

They both transformed back and forth once more, grinning like kids with a new toy.

Then came flight.

Nick lifted awkwardly at first, wobbling, arms flailing.

“Easy, Cub,” Mike coached calmly. “Think balance, not force.”

Nick steadied.

Hovered.

Then rose smoothly.

“I’m doing it!”

“You’re doing it bear-illiantly,” Mike said.

Nick groaned mid-air. “Stop encouraging yourself.”

They flew side by side above the rooftop, laughing, sunlight glinting off rainbow trails.

On his first landing attempt, Nick came down too hard.

The HVAC unit crumpled beneath his feet like a soda can.

Nick froze in horror.

“I—Mike—I’m so sorry—”

Mike landed beside him instantly. “Hey. It’s okay.”

Nick stared at the wreckage. “I broke the building.”

“You adjusted,” Mike said gently. “That’s part of learning.”

He handed Nick an empty soda can from a bench.

“Try holding it without crushing.”

Nick did.

Carefully.

Then dented it slightly.

Then crushed it.

Control settled into his muscles like a new language.

“I get it,” Nick said softly.

“Yeah,” Mike said proudly. “You do.”

---

A sudden metallic echo drifted across the rooftop.

A sharp clang from a loose railing shifting in the wind.

Nick’s breath caught.

The sound twisted into memory.

Cold metal at his back.  
Straps pulling tight.  
The chair biting into bone.

His body went rigid.

Mike noticed instantly.

“Nick.”

Nick swallowed hard.

Images flashed — Evan’s blank eyes, the industrial room, the calm voice that had known exactly what he was doing.

“I keep thinking about Evan,” Nick whispered. “He’s still there.”

Mike placed both hands gently on Nick’s arms, grounding him.

“We’ll save him,” he said steadily. “I promise.”

Nick nodded, breath slowing.

They stood quietly together for a moment.

---

Then Nick straightened.

“We should keep going,” he said.

Mike studied him carefully. “You sure?”

“Yeah,” Nick said. “For Evan. For everyone.”

He lifted higher this time. Faster. Confident.

Joy threaded through the movement.

Not reckless.

Intentional.

Mike followed beside him, smiling wide.

Strength felt good.

Space felt good.

Being alive in his body felt good.

---

Eventually, they landed again, both breathing heavier, pleasantly tired.

They changed back into their suits and sat at the edge of the rooftop, legs dangling over the city.

Nick rested one hand on the concrete, the other drifting naturally to Mike’s thigh.

Mike’s arm wrapped warmly around his shoulders.

Morning light washed the buildings in gold.

“This feels like me,” Nick said quietly.

Mike smiled. “I can see that.”

“I don’t feel lost,” Nick added.

“Good,” Mike said softly. “Because you’re not.”

They sat together in the quiet hum of the city, bodies relaxed, presence easy, the world stretching wide in front of them.

For the first time since everything had changed, Nick felt something settle fully into place.

Not fear.

Not uncertainty.

Belonging.
